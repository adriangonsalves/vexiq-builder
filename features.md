# VEX IQ 3D Robot Modeler — Features & Capabilities (`features.md`)

> **Last Updated:** 2026-11-03  
> **Scope:** Complete user-facing, manual CAD, automated assembly, validation, and AI/MCP capabilities of the VEX IQ 3D Robot Modeler.

## 1. 482-Part VEX IQ CAD Library & Semantic Catalog

- **Complete Official Part Coverage (`482` Parts):**
  - Converted from STEP CAD models (`VEX-IQ-All-Parts` & `VEX-IQ-All-Control`) into centered, millimeter-accurate `.glb` meshes (`public/models/`) and indexed in [`public/catalog.json`](file:///Users/adriangonsalves/projects/vexiq_builder/public/catalog.json) + [`public/part-metadata.json`](file:///Users/adriangonsalves/projects/vexiq_builder/public/part-metadata.json).
  - **Categories:**
    - `Beams & Plates` (1x/2x/4x Beams, Specialty Beams, Corner Connectors, Plates)
    - `Pins & Standoffs` (0x2, 0x3, 1x1, 1x2, 2x2 Capped/Uncapped Connector Pins, Standoffs)
    - `Shafts & Collars` (Pitch Shafts, Capped Shafts, Motor Shafts, Rubber/Set-Screw Collars, Bushings)
    - `Gears & Sprockets` (12T, 24T, 36T, 48T, 60T Spur, Crown, Bevel, Differential, Worm Gears, Sprockets)
    - `Wheels &Hubs` (200mm Omni-Directional, Traction, Balloon Tires, Hubs)
    - `Control & Electronics` (Gen 1 & Gen 2 Robot Brain, Smart Motors, Battery, Optical/Distance/Bumper/Touch LED/Gyro Sensors, Controller)
    - `Pneumatics & Linear Motion` (Pneumatic Cylinders, Solenoids, Air Tanks, Fittings, Linear Racks/Sliders)
  - **Multi-colour parts keep their official colours:** When an official STEP file colours its bodies in more than one colour (like the metal motor shafts with their red stop ring, the 200mm omni wheel with grey hub, black rubber rollers and metal pins, or the Smart Motor, brain, cables and sensors with their multi-tone bodies), the converter keeps each colour body in its STEP colour. The body with the most faces acts as the main body and takes the part's chosen colour, while extra bodies keep their fixed colours (`userData.fixedColor`, `userData.rubber`) in both the 3D viewport and part thumbnails.
  - **Reflective metallic silver shafts:** Non-plastic shafts (motor shafts, capped shafts, standard axles) render with a reflective metallic silver finish (`#e2e8f0`, `metalness: 0.85`, `roughness: 0.22`), distinguishing real steel and aluminium axles from plastic parts. Multi-colour motor shafts retain their red stop ring as a fixed accent.
  - **Black rubber stays black:** Near-black rubber bodies (rollers, shock absorber seals, ropes) are stored with a `"rubber"` material. The 3D view and thumbnails draw them in dark rubber texture whatever colour the part is given.
  - **Parts inside other parts' files:** the Gen 1 Robot Brain's STEP file includes its battery, which is a catalog part of its own, so the converter leaves that body out of the brain (`SEPARATE_BODIES` in `scripts/convert-parts.mjs`). A second catalog entry, **VEX IQ Robot Brain Gen 1 with Battery** (part number `228-2540 + 228-2604`), keeps every body of the file for builds that don't need the battery as its own part (`WHOLE_FILE_VARIANTS`); it has the same side mounting holes. A battery placed at its bay pose in a brain counts as held by the audit (`batteryBays`, `src/utils/batteryBay.js`).
  - **Colours set per face:** newer VEX STEP files (the Gen 2 Robot Brain 228-6480 and Gen 2 Robot Battery 228-7045) colour each face rather than each body; the converter splits such a body into one piece per colour, so the screen surround, buttons and labels keep VEX's colours. For those two parts the main body also keeps its file colour instead of the kit colour (`FILE_COLOR_PART_NUMBERS` in `scripts/convert-parts.mjs`).
  - **Sliding a battery in:** select the Robot Battery, then double-click a Robot Brain: the battery moves into the brain's bay (one history step, "Slide … into …"). The click on the brain waits for the second click instead of selecting the brain. Nothing moves if another part is in the way, and a locked battery stays put.
  - **Spacers and washers:** their centre hole runs their whole thickness, so a shaft can be inserted through them (the CAD file alone gave it no length).
  - **Adding a part:** put its STEP file in `VEX-IQ-All-Parts/`, then run `npm run convert` (only new files are converted) and `npm run index-metadata`. A wheel whose thinnest side is Y (like the 2nd gen omni wheel) gets its centre bore along Y.
- **Live 3D Part Thumbnails & Search:**
  - Interactive category filter pills with part counts.
  - Instant search by part name, VEX part number (`228-2500-...`), or category.
  - **List / Grid:** a switch in the library header. List is one card per row; Grid shows only thumbnails (100 px, three to a row), and each tile shows the part's longest side (for example `190 mm`). The choice is remembered in this browser, not in the build.
  - **Hover:** in List and Grid, resting on a part shows its name, longest side and part number (faint) after a moment, and the 300 px picture after 1 second. Thumbnails are transparent, drawn on a dark tile, and dark parts keep their real shade.
  - **Adding by click lands in view:** clicking a part adds it at a clear spot near the middle of what the camera shows (on the plane through the orbit point facing the camera, on the 1-pitch world grid, not touching any part's box, all of it inside the view), whatever is selected. MCP and other adds that give a position are unchanged.
  - **Drag a part into the view to place it:** press on a card or tile and drag (a chip with its name follows the pointer). Where the pointer enters the 3D view the part is added, selected and starts **placing**: it follows the pointer at its distance from the camera; near a hole face or axle end it fits (the hole-picking targets) it sits at that hole's first fit, the wheel / `R` / `Shift+R` step through the fits, and a click puts it in (pushed in like mating). Letting go of the drag keeps placing, so the pointer can carry on. A click away from any hole puts it down where it is; `Esc` (or another selection, Move/Rotate, Explode) takes it away again. Parts with no pins, shafts or bores (beams, plates, the brain) snap onto the part under the pointer: its free pin, peg and shaft ends first (a corner hole slid on), else its free holes (a corner hole lying on the hole's face), nearest the pointed spot; targets the part can't fit are skipped, the wheel / `R` steps through the positions (kept while the pointer stays on that part), and a hole or pin end within reach of the pointer wins. A hole whose rim sits below the part's surface (a corner connector's) still takes a beam lying on it: it backs off up to 3 mm to clear the surface, then pushes in to touch. Double-clicking a placed beam with nothing lined up starts the same picking (point at a part, wheel for positions, click to put it on). Over empty space they follow the pointer and pull onto lined-up holes like a drag (not with Alt). Adding and placing is one history step ("Add … into …" or "Add …"); a cancelled part leaves no step. Dragged-in parts count toward Recent.
  - **Recent:** the first filter pill holds up to 20 parts added from the library (by click or drag), ranked by a score that rises with each use and fades with every later add, so parts used often and lately stay on top. Parts placed by MCP, duplicated, copy-inserted or loaded don't count. Kept in this browser, the same for every build.
  - On-demand WebGL thumbnail rendering ([`src/components/PartThumbnail.jsx`](file:///Users/adriangonsalves/projects/vexiq_builder/src/components/PartThumbnail.jsx)) with viewport visibility deferral (`content-visibility: auto`).
  - 72 px thumbnails (rendered at 2x) on a light tile, framed to each part's size as seen from the thumbnail angle (short parts aren't cropped, long racks aren't tiny). Very dark parts are lifted to a mid tone in thumbnails only (the 3D view keeps real colors) and every part gets dark outlines, extra dense on near-white connectors, so holes and edges read. A thumbnail is redrawn whenever its part's default color changes. Long part names wrap to two lines.
- **Assembly Outliner search and base name:** a search box under the Outliner title filters rows by label, catalog part name or part number (the title shows shown / total while searching; `Esc` or the clear button empties it). It only changes which rows are listed: selection, 3D view, history and Clear All are untouched. The selected part's row, and the top of the Inspector, show its catalog name and part number (for example "VEX IQ Smart Motor · 228-2560"), so a part with its own label can still be traced to its catalog part.
- **4,228 BVH-Verified Semantic Connection Ports:**
  - Every part is indexed with typed, raycast-verified local connection ports:
    - `pin_hole` (Cyan) — $\varnothing 4.8\text{ mm}$ round pin holes with through/blind classification and axial span `spanMm`.
    - `shaft_bore` (Pink) — Square shaft through-bores on gears, wheels, pulleys, and lock plates.
    - `shaft_socket` (Purple) — Blind square shaft sockets inside Smart Motors and Brains.
    - `pin_peg` (Yellow) — Insertable cylindrical pegs on Connector Pins and Standoffs with shoulder/cap geometry (`fastenerSpecs`).
    - `shaft` — every shaft's own axis, both `ends`, its cross-section (`shape`) and, for capped and motor shafts, which end has the cap or stop ring (`stop`).
  - **Stable ids:** each point's `portId` is made from its kind, the face it opens on and its position in quarter pitches, such as `hole.+y.-2_8_-2` (a pin hole on the +Y face; `thru` for a through hole, `#2` on a clash), so ids don't change when parts are indexed again. The id each point had before (`hole_14`, `motor_drive_socket`, `shaft`) is kept in `aliases`, and every tool and action that takes a port id accepts either; replies give the stable id. Each point also has `kind`, `shape` (`round`, `square`) and `faces`.
  - **Points the index finds:** holes on any face too shallow for a whole pin leg (the Bumper Switch's four top holes), a blind hole's opening on the face a pin actually goes into, the pegs of intake flaps and grippers (from the Tank Tread Attachment Link's holes and each part's `seatInLink`, so pegs and the track builder agree) and the link's two attachment holes, and the Mini Standoff Connector's bore as a pin hole. A re-index that would drop an old id fails.
- **Measured gear, rack and square-shaft data** (`scripts/measure-part-geometry.mjs`, run by `npm run index-metadata`): rays cast at the real mesh every 0.25° and fitted for their repeating pattern, so the values come from the models, not the names. Angles are about each axis from a shared reference direction (`src/utils/axisFrame.js`), so the app and the MCP engine read them the same way.
  - Gears that mesh at 90° (openspec right-angle-gears): bevel gears get their cone point (the tip and root lines must agree on it within 0.5 mm; snapped to the pitch grid within 0.4 mm), tooth phase and face span; the 36 Tooth Crown Gear its toothed face (−z), pitch plane, tooth ring radii, tooth size and phase (its rim is smooth); the Worm Gear its thread spacing, lead, number of threads, winding direction (`hand`), threaded length, thread phase and pitch radius (6.35 mm: tip minus one module, snapped to the grid). The Differential Gear housings aren't measured.
  - Spur gears (`gearSpecs`): `measured`, tooth count, tip and root radius, tooth size (`diametralPitch` 16 for high strength, 24 standard, 34 for the 34DP pair) and from it `moduleMm`, `pitchRadiusMm`, `toothSpacingMm`, the tooth phase `toothPhaseDeg` (the angle of one tooth's center), the tooth band along the axis `faceSpanMm`, and `boreShape` (square or round, e.g. the idler gear is round). Ratchets, ring gears and toothed non-gears are left unmeasured and never turn to mesh; the indexer lists them.
  - Linear motion racks (`rackSpecs`): length direction, tooth face normal, tooth axis, tooth spacing and the offset of one tooth, tip and pitch lines, length and tooth band.
  - Shaft bores and sockets get `shape` (`square` / `round`) and the square's flat angle `squarePhaseDeg`; square shafts get `fastenerSpecs.shape` and `squarePhaseDeg`. A bore that can't be measured counts as round.
  - The Smart Motor gets `hubSpecs` (`radiusMm`, `faceOffsetMm`, `depthMm`): the drive hub around its square socket, the piece that turns.

## 2. Interactive 3D Viewport & Physical Contact Snapping

- **VEX Pitch Grid (`1x Pitch = 12.7 mm = 0.5"`):**
  - Dual-resolution floor grid (`12.7 mm` major lines in blue, `6.35 mm` half-pitch minor lines in slate).
  - Selectable pitch snap increments in the toolbar's **Snap** drop-down: **`1P` (`12.7 mm`)**, **`0.5P` (`6.35 mm`)**, **`0.25P` (`3.175 mm`)**, and **`Free`**.
  - Rotation snap setting (`15°`, `45°`, `90°`, or `Free`), kept in the saved viewport state and settable by the assistant (there is no toolbar control for it yet).
- **Move Gizmo: Axis Arrows + Plane-Drag Handles:**
  - Selecting a part in Move mode (`W`) shows red/green/blue **axis arrows** (drag along `X`, `Y`, or `Z`) plus three large, outlined, color-coded **plane squares**:
    - **Blue `XY` square** — drag within the front/back (vertical) plane; `Z` stays fixed.
    - **Red `YZ` square** — drag within the side (vertical) plane; `X` stays fixed.
    - **Green `XZ` square** — drag along the ground plane; height `Y` stays fixed.
  - The hovered/active plane highlights yellow. Plane handles auto-hide when viewed edge-on (where in-plane dragging would be unstable).
  - Plane drags use the same VEX pitch snapping and BVH flush-contact detent as axis drags.
  - **The Move arrows follow the selected part's own axes** (local), so a part lined up with a beam at an odd angle slides parallel to it, in whole pitch steps along the arrow. Hold **`Alt`** to show the world's X / Y / Z arrows instead; the arrows switch when Alt goes down or up (not in the middle of a drag). Groups and multi-selections always use the world's axes.
  - **Pitch steps count from where the drag started**, not from the world origin. A part that starts lined up with holes, even holes off the world grid like axles at wheel-radius height (`y = 23.87 mm`), moves in whole steps and stays on the hole pattern.
  - **Hole snapping:**
    - While dragging, a pin, shaft or hole of the moving part that comes within `3.5 mm` of a matching hole, pin or shaft on another part is pulled onto that part's axis line. The two must be parallel within `2°` and within 12 pitch (`152.4 mm`) of each other along the line, the same reach as the hole rings, so a gear dragged well above a beam still snaps onto the hole below. When two pulls are within `0.25 mm` of each other, the hole nearer along the line wins.
    - A small sideways miss of up to `1 mm`, on axes not being dragged or when dragging along the hole's line, is settled too. A part that drifted slightly off a hole pattern then clicks back into its hole, for example after snapping into a gear's pin hole that's turned for meshing.
    - Apart from that settle, the pull only moves the part along the axes being dragged.
    - It works at any angle, because it uses the real hole lines, not the grid. Plates turned in their own plane or tilted on another axis snap the same way.
    - Dragged axes that run along the hole's line stay on the pitch grid, so a shaft can still be slid in and out in steps.
    - **Holes pull onto holes too:** a hole of the moving part pulls onto a hole of another part when a shaft would fit both (pin holes, shaft bores, the Smart Motor's drive socket, rubber collar holes). This lines up a corner connector or a beam against a beam, a gear's bore over a beam hole, or a motor's socket over a beam hole for an axle through the beam. The same 3.5 mm / 2 pitch / 1 mm settle rules apply.
    - **Small tilts are straightened:** when the nearest matching pair is between 0.1° and 5° off parallel, the part first turns by that angle, about its matched pin or hole, so they are parallel, then pulls on. Pairs that agree on one turn win; 5° or more is left alone. Worked out from the drag's start turn each frame, so it never adds up.
    - Hold **`Alt`** while dragging to move without hole snapping (the arrows then show the world's axes too).
  - **Shaft caps don't pass through holes:**
    - Collision meshes are shrunk `0.38 mm` per part so pins and shafts fit their holes. That hid small overlaps, such as a capped shaft's cap (radius about `2.37 mm`) in a beam hole (about `2.10 mm`).
    - Each shaft part's width along its length and each hole's widest pass-through are now measured on the real mesh. A shaft lined up with a hole (within `1 mm`) collides when a section of it inside the hole is wider than the hole lets through.
    - This applies to drags, nudges, MCP placement and the audit.
    - Square shafts in square gear bores and shafts in rubber collars are not affected.
    - Drags and nudges also check the path, so a whole grid step can't carry a cap past a hole's lip: the shaft stops with the cap resting on the lip.
- **Rotate Mode: Rings Around the Part:**
  - **Right-click the selected part** (it can be behind another part) to switch between Move and Rotate; `E` and the context bar's **Rotate** button open the same mode, `W` / **Move** leave it. Right-drag still pans the camera, and the browser menu never opens over the viewport.
  - A part that isn't held by anything gets three flat rings, **red `X`**, **green `Y`** and **blue `Z`**, centered on the middle of the part. The rings follow the **part's own axes** (like the Move arrows), so a part set at an odd angle turns about its own length or width; hold **`Alt`** for rings on the world's axes (they switch when Alt goes down or up, not during a turn). Dragging a ring turns the part around that axis through its center, so any orientation can be reached. Groups and multi-selections always use the world's axes.
  - A part **held by a pin or shaft** (one of its pins or its shaft is inside another part's hole, or another part's pin or shaft is inside one of its holes) gets **one orange ring** around that joint's axis. Dragging it swings the part around the pin or shaft, which stays in its hole. Joints on the same axis line (a shaft through a beam and a gear, a pin through two stacked beams) count as one.
  - A part held on **two or more axis lines** gets the X, Y and Z rings like a free part. When those lines are parallel and lie in one row (a corner connector's two pegs in a beam), the rings turn about a point on the line along the row, at the middle of where the pins sit in their holes, and the ring along the row turns exactly about it (a fourth ring when no ring runs that way): a half turn on it flips the part over with every pin back in its hole (openspec precise-placement). Any turn (one part or a set) that ends overlapping a part it wasn't already overlapping is put back on release, with "Can't turn there: it would overlap another part. Put back." and no history step. Locked parts ("Part is locked") and Explode view ("Can't rotate in Explode view") are refused.
  - Rings always go around the whole part and keep a steady size on screen when zooming out. Tick marks show the snap angles; while dragging, the turned slice is shaded.
  - Each ring has an **angle label** (e.g. `Z 30°`, `↻ 45°` for a joint) counting the turn since Rotate mode started for this part, shown from `−180°` to `180°`. Dragging snaps to the rotation snap setting (`Free` follows the pointer). **Click a label to type an angle**; `Enter` turns the part to exactly that angle, `Esc` or clicking away cancels.
  - **Click a ring** to select it (it brightens): the **mouse wheel** then turns the part one snap step per notch (`5°` when `Free`) instead of zooming. Click the ring again, click elsewhere, or press `Esc` to unselect it.
  - **Turning line:** hovering, selecting or dragging a ring draws the line the part will turn about, in the ring's colour (for the Joint ring, the joint's own line); while the ring is selected or dragged the line is thick with a soft glow. Anchor lines are thinner, and the one under the pointer thickens.
  - **Turn about a pin or hole (anchor):** for one part showing the X, Y and Z rings (free, or held on two or more axis lines), selecting a ring highlights in orange a line through every one of the part's holes, pins and shafts that runs the same way as that ring (within `2°`), the lines that hold the part drawn stronger (openspec precise-placement). **Click an orange line** to move the rings onto it: the part stays put, the ring stays selected, and dragging, typing or the wheel now swing the part about that pin or shaft. A **dashed line** marks the part's usual centre; click it to move the rings back. The angle labels keep counting. The chosen line is kept between turns and forgotten when the selection changes or Rotate mode is entered again; groups and multi-selections always turn about their centre.
  - Turning isn't blocked while the ring moves: an overlap is shown as it happens. Every finished drag, typed angle, or pause in wheel turning that ends clear is saved and synced like a drag release, and which joints hold the part is worked out again; one that ends in a new overlap is put back.
- **BVH Physical Flush Contact Boundary Snapping (Contact Detent):**
  - When moving parts with the **3D Move Gizmo** (`W`) or **Inspector Nudge Buttons** (`-X/+X`, `-Y/+Y`, `-Z/+Z`), the translation solver automatically detects physical contact boundaries using a **10-step BVH binary search** + **`0.25P` (`3.175 mm`) sub-pitch flush seat detection**.
  - Pushing a connector pin into a beam or plate stops **flush** when the pin's flat cap/shoulder contacts the plate surface (`z = ±3.175 mm`) instead of stepping into the solid wall of the beam.
  - Continuing to drag past the contact detent releases the part so it can pass through to the other side when desired.
- **Aligned Hole Highlighting (See-Through):**
  - While a part is selected or dragged (Move `W` or Rotate `E`), every opening that lines up with one of its pins or its shaft is marked with an **orange ring** on the hole's rim, projected onto the receiving part's actual surface on the side where the pin or shaft will enter (e.g. the recessed floor around a beam hole), shown as a **solid ring on the surface at both faces** of the receiving part (only the entry face for the blind motor socket), plus a **faint see-through ring** at the entry face that shows through any parts in front, so alignment is visible without changing the camera view. Rings match each opening's rim (pin holes `3.175 mm`, bores and the motor socket `2.5 mm`, collar hole `1.575 mm`).
  - **Pins** (`pin_peg`) light up round **pin holes** (`pin_hole`). **Shafts** light up pin holes, **gear/wheel/pulley bores** (`shaft_bore`), the **Smart Motor drive socket** (`shaft_socket`), and the hole in a **Rubber Shaft Collar**.
  - Works both ways: dragging a beam, gear, wheel, motor, or rubber collar lights up its own openings that line up with pins or shafts already placed.
  - "Lined up" means axes within `2°`, axis lines within `0.25 mm`, and the opening within `12P` (`152.4 mm`) of the pin or shaft along the axis.
  - Pins only project the way they stick out of their part: each pin end is checked against the 3D model, so a corner connector no longer lights holes on its body side, while connector pins, idler pins (snap end and smooth end) and male–male standoffs project both ways. Every pin part works, including the 2x3 Smooth Idler Pin, which has no pin in the catalog data.
  - A ring shows only while the pin or shaft is still outside that opening; it disappears as soon as the pin or shaft reaches the surface the ring is drawn on (more openings further along the same line stay lit until reached). Through holes in beams, plates, and gears work from either side.
  - Rings stay while the part remains selected (including after Inspector nudges, Snap & Mate, and MCP updates) and clear on deselect, when the part is hidden, or in Explode view. Highlighting never moves parts or changes saved state.
- **Double-Click Mating:**
  - With rings showing, **double-click the selected part** (it can be behind another part) to finish the joint: it is shifted sideways onto the hole's axis (at most `0.25 mm`), then moved straight along the axis until it touches the receiving part (pin cap or shoulder on the face, shaft at the bottom of the motor socket), using the same flush-contact stop as dragging. It keeps going while the pin or shaft is still in the holes, so a pin goes all the way in even after its tip comes out the far side. The saved position is checked so the part never ends up overlapping. It is never rotated.
  - Works both ways: a pin or shaft goes into the lined-up holes, or a beam, gear, or motor moves onto lined-up pins and shafts. Holes lined up in the same direction mate together.
  - **Holes lined up with holes** (hole to hole, when a shaft would fit both) also get rings while the parts are apart. Double-clicking **stacks** the part along the hole axis until it rests on the other part ("Stacked on …"), ready to pin. The rings go away once the parts touch (closer than about `0.9 mm`, the collision check's contact tolerance).
  - **A part with both pins and holes:**
    - When its pins and its holes line up in different directions, double-click shows one arrow per direction.
    - When only its holes line up and its pins line up with nothing, double-click shows the stacking arrow and starts hole picking for the pins at the same time. Click the arrow to stack, or point at any hole and click to insert the part by a pin there. `Esc` or an empty click cancels both.
  - If nothing is touched while the pin or shaft is in the holes (a plain shaft through beams and gears, a gear onto a shaft), the part stops when the leading end is flush with the far face of the last lined-up hole.
  - Parts that can mate in more than one direction (e.g. the **Triple 2x Wide, 2x2 Corner Connector**, with pins on `+X`, `+Z` and `−Z`) show **one large orange arrow per direction** instead, and the gizmo hides. **Click an arrow** to mate that way; `Esc`, clicking elsewhere, or a selection change cancels.
  - The result is saved and synced like a drag release, and a message appears in the Inspector ("Mated with …", "Nothing lined up to mate with", "Part is locked", "Can't mate: already touching another part").
- **Pick a hole (double-click with nothing lined up):**
  - Double-clicking a selected part that has pins, pegs or shafts but lines up with nothing starts **hole picking**. A cyan banner shows the controls and the gizmo hides; the camera still moves.
  - **Hover** near a hole face, within about `24 px`, to ring it in cyan. The face you point at is the side the part goes in from. A hole that already has a pin or shaft in it rings grey and says so.
  - **Bore parts** (gears, wheel hubs, pulleys, sprockets, the Smart Motor: no pins or shafts, but a shaft bore or socket) start picking the same way. Their targets are the two ends of other parts' axles (the part slides onto the axle until it touches something) and hole faces a shaft fits (the bore lines up with the hole and the part rests on the face). The preview is turned to fit a square axle, and a gear is shown at its meshing turn when it meshes along its way in. The banner leaves out the pin count, and an axle end is never "taken". Ctrl/Cmd copy insert works for them too.
  - A **see-through cyan copy** shows the part turned onto the hole's axis whatever angle it was at, its tip `1 mm` outside the face. Its roll follows the receiving part: a pattern on a plate turned 30° is shown turned 30°.
  - Other pins pointing the same way are marked **green** (a hole lines up) or **red** (none does). The banner reads, for example, "Fit 1 of 8 · 2 of 2 pins in holes".
  - The best fit (most pins in holes, then the smallest turn) comes first. The **mouse wheel** or **`R`** / **`Shift+R`** steps through the others: which pin goes in the hole, which end leads, 90° rolls, and **pattern rolls**. A pattern roll is the exact turn that puts another pin on a parallel hole. Holes are searched out to the part's own widest pin spread across the axis and within 2 pitch along it, so hole rows that aren't square to their part (like the angled face of a 30° corner connector) can still be filled. Pins and shafts that look the same at any roll get one roll; capped shafts always lead with the uncapped end.
  - **Click** to insert. The part is turned and placed, then pushed in until it touches, exactly like double-click mating (a shaft cap stops on the hole's lip). It is saved as one history step, "Insert … into …", so one undo restores it. Something in the way just outside the hole gives "The way in is blocked".
  - `Esc`, clicking where no hole is ringed, selecting another part, changing Move/Rotate or Explode, or the part being moved by undo or MCP ends picking without changes.
  - Parts without pins or shafts (beams, plates) still get "Nothing lined up to mate with". Picking a beam onto pins already in the model is a planned follow-up.
- **Quick copy insert (Ctrl/Cmd + double-click a hole):**
  - With a part that has pins, pegs or shafts selected, **hold Ctrl** (or **Cmd** on macOS) and point near a hole on any other part. The same hover and fits as hole picking are shown: a see-through copy at the best fit, green/red pin markers, and a **cyan ring on every hole the copy's pins would go into** (both holes for a two-peg corner connector). A hole that already has a part in it rings grey. The gizmo stays; a short status line names the part and the fit.
  - The **mouse wheel** steps through other fits. `R` isn't used, because Ctrl+R / Cmd+R reloads the browser.
  - **Ctrl/Cmd + double-click** adds a new copy, pushed in until it touches like a hole-picking insert. The original doesn't move and stays selected, so more copies can be stamped straight away. Each copy is named after its catalog part with the next free number ("0x2 Connector Pin #7"), keeps the original's color, and is one history step, "Insert copy of … into …".
  - A taken hole ("That hole already has a part in it") or a blocked way in ("The way in is blocked") adds nothing.
  - The double-click is read from the two presses, so it works on macOS where Ctrl+click arrives as a right-click. Clicks with Ctrl/Cmd held during the preview never change the selection or switch Move/Rotate, and a Ctrl/Cmd double-click never mates.
  - Letting go of the key, leaving the window, changing the selection or Explode ends the preview without changes. It doesn't run during a drag, hole picking or while mate arrows are shown.
- **Target hole or pin (Alt + click), then add from the library** (openspec target-hole-placement):
  - **Hold Alt** (Option on macOS) over the 3D view: the hole face or free pin, peg or shaft end nearest the cursor, on any visible part, gets a magenta ring. Nothing needs to be selected. A hole that already has a part in it rings grey. Shift+Alt doesn't light anything (Shift+Alt+click still picks one part of a group). Holding Alt also shows the Move arrows along the world's axes, as before.
  - **Alt+click** makes it the **target**: the magenta ring stays after Alt is let go, and the selection doesn't change. Alt+click another hole or end to move it, or the target itself (or `Esc`) to clear it. It follows its part when the part moves, and goes when the part is hidden or removed or something is put into it. It isn't saved.
  - **Click a part in the library** while a target is set: the part appears already in the target, seated, at its best fit, and stays there while you choose. Pins, pegs and shafts plug into a target hole; a part with holes slides onto a target pin or shaft end; a gear, hub or sprocket goes on by its bore; a beam or plate with nothing to plug in lies flat on the target face with one of its holes over the target hole, ready for pins. Beams and plates (more than four holes facing one way) only use their **corner holes** for this: a beam's two end holes, a plate's four corners.
  - Only fits whose way in is clear are offered, and plug fits come before hole-on-hole ones. The **mouse wheel**, `R` and `Shift+R` step through them; the banner says "Fit 2 of 6".
  - **Click** in the view to keep it: one history step, "Add … into …", and the target clears. `Esc` (or selecting another part) takes the part away again with no history step and keeps the target. Clicking another library part swaps it in. When no fit is free, nothing is added and a message says so.
  - **Double-click a part already in the build** while a target is set on another part (or select it and tap Join, which then reads Auto Join with the tip "Put the part into the marked hole"): it is shown at its best free fit in the target, before anything it happens to line up with, with the same wheel / `R` stepping. A click keeps it as one step, "Move … into …", and the target clears; `Esc` leaves it where it was and keeps the target. No free fit: "No free fit for … at this hole" (or "on this pin" / "on this shaft"), nothing moves (openspec precise-placement).
- **Real-Time Concave Mesh Collision Detection (`three-mesh-bvh`):**
  - Uses inward-eroded (`0.38 mm` clearance) concave triangle meshes so pins and shafts pass cleanly through `4.8 mm` holes without false-positive bounding-box collisions, while true solid-body intersections are flagged immediately.
  - A **tire on its hub** isn't an overlap: tires are moulded smaller than the hub they stretch over, so a tire whose axis lines up with a hub's and whose width covers the hub's middle is left out of the check (`src/utils/tireFit.js`, shared by the app and the MCP engine).
  - **Tight fits are not overlaps:** when two parts meet, they are tested again with each surface pulled in a further `0.5 mm`. If they no longer meet, the contact is a **tight fit**, as VEX's own CAD has many of (a few tenths of a millimetre): the part isn't marked overlapping, and the Inspector, MCP replies (`tightFits`) and the audit list it apart. Moves that stop at first contact (inserting, sliding along a hole line, Fix assembly) still stop at a tight fit. The app, the MCP engine and the audit use the same rule (`TIGHT_FIT_MM` in `src/engine/core.js`).
  - Intersecting parts render in **transparent grey** (`opacity: 0.42`) with a top HUD warning naming what the part passes into, and a one-click **Revert Pose / Cancel Placement** button.
  - Only the part the user is placing in the app (a library drop, a drag, a hole-pick insert, a copy or clone) goes back to its `lastValidPosition` / `lastValidRotation`, or is removed when it has none, once the selection changes. A part placed by MCP, imported from CAD, or brought back by undo, redo or the timeline keeps its pose and its overlap warning until it is moved, fixed or removed.
- **Explode (openspec explode-view):** **Tools → Explode** (ticked while the panel is open) or `X` opens a small panel. It always takes the robot apart every way the parts come apart (there is no X / Y / Z choice). While that's being worked out, the panel shows a ring filling up with a percentage; then a slider from 0% (built) to 100% (fully apart) appears and plays from 0% to 100% over about 2.5 s. Pressing the slider stops the play there; a save from another open tab doesn't. Closing the panel (its ×, Tools → Explode, `X` or `Esc`) puts every part back.
  - **Spread out, for big or locked builds:** a build of more than 300 visible parts, or one the plan below leaves mostly in place (fewer than half its parts moved, as on Flop, whose parts are pinned in from several sides), is spread out instead, at once: up to 40% of the slider the groups move apart as wholes, then each piece (a part with its pins, a tire on its hub, a whole chain) moves straight out from its group's middle, further the further out it starts, sideways or up but never down through the floor. Fully apart, no two parts overlap: a piece still meeting another at 100% is pushed further out along its way (the one of the two whose way leads away from the other) until nothing meets (about 5 s on Flop). On the way out parts may pass through each other. A build without groups spreads its pieces from the robot's middle over the whole slider.
  - **Parts come apart in a real order, with nothing passing through anything.** The bridge works out how the robot comes apart (`src/utils/explodePlan.js`, run in a worker by the dev server): each piece slides out in a straight line along a pin or shaft axis it sits on when that way is clear, otherwise along a main axis, carrying the parts held on it. A shaft's stop ring or cap never goes through a hole it can't pass. The brain (or the largest part) stays put. Pieces held on the brain move first; the parts held on them spread from them after, so a piece never leaves its holder before the holder moves. A lump of parts that only comes apart once it is away from the rest (a side frame) is taken apart again on its own.
  - **No overlaps at any slider value:** every start time is checked against the motion of everything else at each 1% of the slider, with the same shrunk meshes and stop-ring test the audit uses. A piece that can't come off cleanly any way stays on the part it is held on. Pairs that overlap as built (a tire on its hub) don't count.
  - **What moves as one:** a group; a pin or standoff and the part it sits deeper in (the larger part on a tie); a tire and its hub; a chain or track (its links and grippers, grouped or not; it slides off its sprockets in one piece). Shafts move on their own; the battery slides out of the brain's bay. Hidden parts are left out.
  - **Direction choice:** `X`, `Y` or `Z` slides pieces only along that axis; pieces that can't go that way stay on their holder. `All` uses every way out.
  - The same slider value always shows the same picture. Moving the slider never changes saved positions or adds a history step; parts keep their rotation. No lines are drawn back to the parts' built spots (the motion shows where they came from). Selecting, hiding, the camera, `F` and the 4-View side views use the shown positions; Move, Rotate, hole picking and the copy preview stay blocked while the panel is open. Any change to the build (hide, undo, an MCP edit) works the explode out again at the same slider value; "Working out explode…" shows while that takes more than a moment, with the ring and percentage in place of the slider.
  - The direction, slider value and last direction are saved with the view; a build saved with the panel open comes back at its slider value without playing.

The view keeps its target, direction and size at the target when switching, and every tool (drag snapping, mating, hole picking, the copy preview, Rotate mode, Explode, Frame) works the same in both. Click a face for that axis view, an edge for the view halfway between two faces, or a corner for an even three-quarter view; hovering highlights the part under the pointer. **Double-click** a face, edge or corner to look from the opposite side. **Drag** on the cube to turn the view (as dragging empty space does; a press that moves under 5 px is a click). **Right-click** it for the view menu: Perspective / Orthographic, Single / 4-View / Stereo (with Cross-eyed, Depth and Full screen in Stereo), and the Grid and Axes switches. Clicks in its square but off the cube reach the parts behind it.

They follow the build (moves, drags, Explode, hidden parts, the selection highlight) and frame all visible parts until zoomed (wheel, about the pointer) or panned (drag); `F` reframes them. Clicks there never select or edit, and the gizmo, rings, arrows, hole rings, pick previews and the cube are drawn only in the working view.

The view cube stays hidden in Stereo.

Where the browser refuses full screen (e.g. inside a frame) the pair still covers the whole window. A hint ("Esc to exit full screen") fades after about 3 s. Editing works as in Stereo. `Esc` ends hole picking, mate arrows or a selected ring first; otherwise it leaves full screen without deselecting, as does switching the layout (also over MCP). Full screen isn't saved.
  - **Wide view (`Wide` / `Panels` toggle in the toolbar):** hides the parts library and Inspector so the viewport takes the full width under the toolbar, in any layout, including Stereo without full screen. Remembered in this browser across reloads (not part of the build).
  - **Shadows (Settings → Display, off by default):** the main light casts soft, subtle shadows (a 4096² map fitted each frame to what the main view sees in Single and Stereo, so zooming in sharpens them, and to the whole build in 4-View; steady while orbiting; a small offset keeps flat faces clean with only a minimal lit gap at inside corners), so parts shade each other, and a dark floor patch fading out at its edge appears under the lowest part to catch the build's shadow. With shadows on the light shifts a little toward the main light for more contrast between faces; with them off the picture is exactly as before. Overlays (gizmo, rings, arrows, previews, cube) never cast. Shadows show in every projection and layout and in `current` screenshots; the map is drawn once per frame however many panes there are.

Uses three's GTAO pass per pane (main, small views, stereo halves) with the overlays drawn on top afterwards, so the gizmo, rings and arrows are never darkened and the see-through pick preview still hides behind parts. Works with or without Shadows, in every projection and layout; off gives exactly the old picture. It costs noticeably more GPU time than Shadows (most in 4-View, which runs it in all four views). MCP screenshots don't include AO.

With Shadows on, every pane keeps the main view's light, which the shadow is drawn for.

### Context bar (openspec context-toolbar)
- **Where:** a column of icon buttons on the right edge of the 3D view, under the view cube (in 4-View, at the working view's right edge, left of the small views; on a phone, on the left edge under the warning button, clear of the cube, with its read-out, tips and menus opening to its right). It ends above the history timeline, the Audit panel or the shortcuts strip, and when the view is short it uses more columns instead of hiding buttons; on a phone turned sideways it sits beside the cube. Buttons are 32 px on desktop and 44 px on tablets, phones and any touch screen. A press on the bar never selects, turns the camera or ends a picking session. Hidden in Play mode, while the Explode panel is open, while a past step is shown from the timeline, and in Stereo full screen.
- **Buttons, by situation** (top to bottom; a button that doesn't apply isn't shown, one that can't act right now is greyed and its tip says why):
  - always: **Move**, **Rotate** (`W`, `E`, right-click the selection; greyed for a strung band), **Add to selection** (Shift + click; the same switch as Select → Select mode), **Mark a hole** (Alt + click);
  - with parts selected: **Auto Join** or **Manual Join** (double-click). It is **Auto Join**, with its own icon (a pin with a double arrow into a slot), while the selection is lined up with something to push into or is a Robot Battery that fits a brain: it mates, shows mate arrows, or slides the battery into the nearest Gen 1 brain with a free bay. Otherwise it is **Manual Join** (a beam's holes with a pointer): it starts hole picking. The name follows as the part is dragged into or out of line; greyed "Part is locked" for a locked part. Also **Grow selection** (Shift + wheel), **Duplicate** (`D`) and **Delete** (`Del`, last, below a divider; no question, Undo brings it back);
  - one part with a pin, peg or shaft, or a bore part: **Stamp copies** (Ctrl/Cmd + double-click);
  - one part showing its arrows or rings (not locked, hidden or a strung band): **World arrows** (Alt: arrows and rings follow the world's axes);
  - Move mode: **Free move** (Alt while dragging: no pull onto holes, no straightening, no meshing; pitch steps still apply); Rotate mode: **Turn step** (15°, 45°, 90°, Free; the rotation snap, which had no control before);
  - a whole group selected: **One part of a group** (Shift + Alt + click).
- **How they behave:** Mark a hole and One part of a group are armed for one use (a tap on a hole marks it, moves the mark or clears it, without changing the selection; a taken hole keeps it armed with the usual message; a tap away from holes does nothing; then the next tap on a part picks just that part). Stamp copies stays on: one click on a lit free hole inserts a copy and the selection never changes; it turns off on a second tap, `Esc`, a selection change, Explode or Play. World arrows and Free move stay as set across selection changes and `Esc`. `Esc` turns off an armed tool first. None of the switches is saved, sent to other tabs or put in the history; all are off after a reload.
- **Keys light buttons:** while Shift is held, Add to selection and Grow selection show on; Alt: Mark a hole, World arrows, Free move; Ctrl/Cmd: Stamp copies; Shift + Alt: One part of a group only. Letting go or leaving the window puts them back; a button tapped on stays on.
- **Tips:** resting the mouse on a button for half a second (or keyboard focus) shows its name, one line on what it does and its keys (e.g. Stamp copies: Ctrl/Cmd + double-click). On a touch screen, pressing and holding shows the name and line, and lifting doesn't press it.
- **The wheel bar:** whenever the mouse wheel steps through something instead of zooming, the buttons give way to a thumb wheel seen edge-on with a read-out beside it, a step button at each end, and the session's tick and cross. Dragging the wheel steps once per 24 px (up is next), with no spin after the finger lifts and no camera movement; the mouse wheel over the view keeps working and turns the bar's wheel, and the mouse wheel over the bar's wheel steps too. With nothing to step (no hole lit, one fit) the wheel is greyed and the read-out says what to do ("Tap a hole").
  - **Fits** (hole picking, placing a library part, a part shown at the marked hole, Stamp copies, or Ctrl/Cmd held with a hole lit): read-out "2 / 6"; tick puts the part in (Stamp copies: inserts a copy and keeps going); cross ends with nothing changed.
  - **Bend fits** (Bend sheet by holes): tick keeps the bend (Enter), cross drops the fit (Esc).
  - **Ring** (a rotate ring selected): one snap step a notch (5° when Free), read-out "Z 45°", tick unselects the ring; Turn step and World arrows stay on the bar.
  - **Layers** (Grow selection tapped): one layer added or taken off a step, read-out "Layer 2 · 48 parts", a **Pins only** switch, tick closes; any other selection change closes it.
  - **Band spots** (band builder with a spot lit): the next spot near the lit one, read-out the spot's name, tick adds it.

In a Fits session or the band builder a tap within about 24 px of a hole, axle end or band spot lights it and shows the fit (or the see-through band); a second tap on it, or the tick, places it; a tap on another one moves the light; a tap near nothing changes nothing (only the cross or `Esc` ends the session); what a tap lit stays lit. ### Selecting several parts and groups

- **Several parts at once:** `Shift`+click in the 3D view adds or removes a part (empty space keeps the selection); a plain click still replaces it. In the Outliner, `Ctrl/Cmd`+click adds or removes a row and `Shift`+click selects every row between the last selected one and the clicked one. The part picked last is the *primary* part, lit more strongly; everything that works on one part (Inspector fields, hole picking, mating, copy-insert, the rotate rings' joint pivot) works when exactly one part is selected. Ctrl/Cmd isn't used for selecting in the 3D view because copy-insert uses it.
- **Selection in the Outliner:** in Select mode every row has a checkbox, and a plain click on a row adds or removes it as in the 3D view (a folder's: the whole group, mixed when only some are selected); outside Select mode a click replaces the selection and the checkboxes are hidden. Shift+click adds a range of rows, each group in it whole, so it selects several groups; Shift+Alt+click adds a range of single parts, inside a group or across groups. In the 3D view, Shift+click adds or removes a part's whole group and Shift+Alt+click just that part. Selected rows stay in their place in the list. Above the list a **Selected (N)** box (open or closed, remembered) lists the selection again (a whole group as its folder, a single part of a group naming its group); clicking a row there shows it in the list, its X unselects it, and the box's buttons hide/show, lock/unlock, colour, clone or delete the whole selection, or Clear it. Selected rows, like selected parts in the 3D view, are amber; the 3D view shows "N parts selected" with a Clear button.
- **Box select** (Select menu or `B`): a left-drag draws a box instead of turning the camera. Left to right (solid box) selects parts wholly inside, right to left (dashed) every part it touches; groups come whole; Shift or Select mode adds. Esc turns it off.
- **Selection look:** selected parts glow amber with amber edges, and get a thin yellow contour with a dark rim around their outline, shown even through parts in front, so they are easy to see whatever their color.
- **Select menu** (toolbar): **Select mode** (a plain click adds or removes parts; the button reads "Select mode" while on; Esc turns it off and keeps the selection; not saved), **Select Connected**, **Select All**, **Select None**, **Invert Selection**, **Group** and **Ungroup** (greyed out when they don't apply).
- **Select Connected** (`C`) selects every part joined to the selection through seated pins, pegs and shafts, with the fasteners between them, using the audit's own joint graph (`jointGraph` in `mcp/assembly-engine.mjs`, fetched through the bridge's `joint_graph` action), so a motor on its shaft, a battery in the brain's bay and meshing gears count as joined, and parts only touching don't (except the audit's clamp rule: a part squeezed between two parts fastened to each other). A message gives the count.
- **Grow or shrink by joints:** with parts selected, `Shift` + mouse wheel over the 3D view adds one layer of directly joined parts per notch (a beam, then its pins, then what the pins go into), and turning it back takes the last layer off, never below where it started; a message shows "Layer N · M parts". It doesn't zoom, and starts again after any other selection change. Joined here also counts meshing gears, a battery in its bay, and parts the audit treats as clamped (touching two parts pinned to each other). **`Shift` + `Ctrl/Cmd` + wheel** grows through pins, shafts and pegs seated in holes only, so parts that merely touch are left out ("Pinned layer N · M parts"); holding `Shift` with `Ctrl/Cmd` doesn't start the copy preview. Switching between the two starts again from the current selection.
- **Groups:** Group (`G`) makes the selection a named component ("Group 1", "Group 2", …); a part already in a group moves to the new one, and a group left with one part goes away. Ungroup (`Shift+G`) breaks up the selected groups. Clicking any part of a group in the 3D view selects the whole group. Groups are stored on the parts (`groupId`, `groupName`), so they are undoable, saved in the build, the library and the history, and kept by the MCP server.
- **Assembly Outliner folders:** each group is a folder row (arrow, name, part count, hide and lock for every part, mixed when only some are), listed above the loose parts; its parts are listed indented while it's open (open folders aren't saved). Clicking a folder selects the group; clicking a row inside selects just that part. The title still counts parts.
- **Locate button** (Outliner header): scrolls the primary part's row into view, opening its folder. Hold it for half a second to pin it: while pinned (shown pressed, remembered in this browser) every new selection, including ones made over MCP, is scrolled to. Hold again to unpin.
- **Moving a set:** a group or any multi-selection moves and turns as one, keeping the parts' places relative to one another: the Move arrows sit at the centre of the parts' combined bounding box, and parts in the set never count as overlapping one another, while outside parts they overlap are marked (openspec group-snapping):
  - **Own hole grid:** drags and nudges move in whole snap steps from where the set is; a centre typed in the Inspector moves it by the whole number of steps nearest the typed value (exact with Free). Turns (rings and quick 90° turns) go about a point on the set's own hole grid near its centre, so quarter turns keep its holes on that grid.
  - **Pulls onto nearby holes:** while dragged, the set's free outer pins, pegs, shafts and holes (not holes filled by its own pins, nor pins buried in its own parts) pull onto matching ones of outside parts with the single-part rules (3.5 mm sideways, agreeing pairs win), but only within **25.4 mm (1 inch) along the line**. A pull that would put the set back where the drag started is refused until it has once been more than 3.5 mm away, so sliding a set off its pins doesn't stick. Pairs up to 5° off parallel straighten the whole set first. The holes it lines up with are highlighted. Alt drags freely.
  - **On release:** a set lined up but pressed in slides along the line to the nearest clear spot; then, if it got more than 3.5 mm from its start and Alt isn't held, it is fitted onto nearby holes with Fix assembly's rules (turned and moved as one piece, at most 25° / 10 mm, then seated), worked out by the engine while the view waits; then, if it overlaps a part it didn't overlap when the drag began, it goes back ("Can't place it there: it would overlap another part. Put back.") and nothing is saved. Otherwise it is one step, e.g. "Move Claw. Lined up 6 pins and holes with Wrist 4x4 Plate".
  - **Double-click mates the set:** double-clicking any selected part of a group or multi-selection mates the whole set. When its free pins and holes are lined up with another part (within 25.4 mm along the line) it is pushed straight in until a part touches, or one mate arrow per way in is shown (click one to push). Otherwise **hole picking for the set** starts, using only its free pins whose free end already points within **30°** of straight into a hole: only those holes can be picked, each fit turns the set by the smallest turn (at most 30°, never round the hole's line) about that pin's tip and slides it to the hole, fits that put the most other pins over holes come first (green / red markers, R or the wheel for the next), and a see-through copy of the whole set shows the fit. Click to push it in ("Insert <set> into <part>"); a way in that is blocked, or a push that would end overlapping something new, leaves the set where it was. With no pin facing a part: "Turn or move the group so its pins face the part to mate it to". Ctrl/Cmd + double-click (copy insert) stays one part at a time.
  - **Part of a group:** parts of a group selected in the Outliner move on their own; group membership doesn't change, and the members left behind count as outside parts (overlaps, put-back, pulls, the fit).
  - **Speed:** the set's pins and holes are collected once per drag; overlap checks test only members whose box meets an outside part's box. A set with a locked or hidden part shows no arrows and says "Part is locked" / "Some parts are hidden"; no arrows in Explode view. Each drag is one step ("Move Left Drive", "Turn 5 parts"). Right-click on any part of a selected group or set switches between Move and Rotate, as for one part. Double-clicking a group mates it (below).

## 2a. Gear Meshing, Square Shafts & the Motor Hub

- **Gears turn to mesh** (`src/utils/gearMeshing.js`): a measured spur gear in mesh range of another gear or a rack turns about its own axis by the smallest turn (at most half a tooth) that puts its teeth in the other's gaps.
  - Mesh range: axes parallel within 2°, tooth bands overlapping along the axis, the same tooth size, and centers within 1.0 mm of the sum of the pitch radii (for a rack: one pitch radius from its pitch line, over its length).
  - While dragging, the gear turns continuously, so tooth clashes no longer hold it short of the hole it is pulled onto; it turns again on release. Alt (no hole snapping) also turns this off.
  - Also on double-click mating, hole picking and Ctrl/Cmd copy insert; for these the turn is worked out where the gear first meshes along its way in, so it can slide down an axle past another gear.
  - With several neighbours, the turn that leaves the largest error smallest wins (ties: the nearest neighbour); any neighbour still more than a quarter tooth off is named ("Couldn't mesh with 36 Tooth Gear #2 as well"). A gear held by a pin off its own axis isn't turned.
  - Turning a gear by hand in Rotate mode is never undone: meshing only happens when a part is placed.
- **Square axles fit square holes** (`src/utils/shaftGroups.js`): a square axle going into a square bore or socket turns by the smallest amount that lines its flats up with the hole's; a part with a square bore going onto an axle turns to fit it. When an axle passes several square holes whose flats disagree, it fits the one it was put into and a message names the others. Round bores take any angle. Hole picking and the copy preview show square axles at the four fitting turns, and a gear at its meshing turn.
- **Shaft groups:** a square axle, every part seated on it by a square bore, and a Smart Motor hub holding it turn together. When a gear placed on a loaded axle meshes, its whole group turns (positions turn about the axle too) and parts on other axles stay put. Round-bore parts (idler gears, bearings) and pin holes aren't in groups.
- **Smart Motor hub:** the motor's drive hub is split off its model when it loads (`src/utils/motorHub.js`) and drawn, and collided, turned by the instance's `socketAngleDeg` (0 when missing, so older saves load unchanged). An axle going into the socket keeps its angle and the hub turns to match. The angle is saved with the build, part of the history, shown by `get_assembly`, and turns with its shaft group.
- **One step:** the placement's own move and every turn it caused (other group members, the hub) are one history step, labeled with what happened, e.g. "Move 24 Tooth Gear #1. Meshed 24 Tooth Gear #1 with 48 Tooth Gear #1 (turned −5.5°)". The same text shows as the action message.
- The app and the MCP engine share the placement step (`src/utils/placementFit.js`), so both place parts identically. The collision meshes (shrunk 0.38 mm) can't see a square axle turned wrong in a square hole (its corners overlap only ~0.6 mm), so square fits are made by angle, not found by collision.

## 2b. Gears at 90°: Bevel, Crown & Worm

- **Pairs that mesh** (`src/utils/gearMeshing.js`): a bevel gear with a bevel gear of the same tooth count (cone points together, axes at 90°); the 36 Tooth Crown Gear with a standard spur gear standing on its toothed face (the spur's axis crossing the crown's, one pitch radius out from its pitch plane, its teeth over the tooth ring); the Worm Gear with a standard spur gear (the worm lying in the gear's tooth plane, the axes the two pitch radii apart, its thread reaching the gear's teeth). Nothing else meshes at 90°. Axes must be at 90° within 2° and distances within 1 mm.
- **Turning to mesh:** one rule for every pair: where the two touch, a tooth of one faces a gap of the other, read along the direction the teeth move there (a worm is read like a rack along its axis, the thread moving one lead per turn). A placed bevel, crown or worm gear, or a spur gear next to a crown or worm, turns about its own axis on every placement spur gears do (drag, double-click mating, hole picking, copy insert, `place_part` / `place_parts`), with its shaft group when it sits on a square axle. Checked against the collision meshes: meshed pairs don't touch, half a tooth off they do (`node scripts/check-right-angle-gears.mjs`).
- **Mesh spots:** a quarter turn apart round the partner, counted from the world axes when the partner's axis runs along one, so a shaft through the gear lines up with the hole grid (4 round a bevel gear, 4 for a spur gear on the crown, 8 for the crown round a spur gear, 4 for a worm round a gear and a gear round a worm). Along a worm, a spot keeps the moved part's place when the meeting point is already on the thread, else the middle of the thread. Spots that hit another part aren't offered.
- **A loose gear jumps into mesh:** dragging a single gear within half a pitch of a free mesh spot on a gear it meshes with at 90° shows a see-through copy there; on release it moves there, turned and meshed, as one history step ("Meshed 18 Tooth Bevel Gear #2 with 18 Tooth Bevel Gear #1"). Nothing needs to hold it: the shaft and beams can be added afterwards. A hole snap wins over a spot; Alt or Free move turn it off. Spur gears beside spur gears don't jump.
- **Just out of mesh snaps in** (openspec precise-placement): a 90° gear placed (dropped, mated, picked, put into the mark, copied, or placed by the assistant) within 3 mm of meshing slides along its own shaft into mesh, and its partner slides along its own shaft too when the first can't close the gap alone (each at most 3 mm; shafts stay put; gears held by anything but their shaft, or locked, don't slide). "Slid 18 Tooth Bevel Gear #2 1.2 mm along its shaft to mesh with 18 Tooth Bevel Gear #1". When the shaft lines themselves miss, nothing slides and Play keeps reporting them out of mesh.
- **Seeing what meshes:** with one gear selected, the gears meshed with it get green edges and 90° gears just out of mesh with it amber ones, following the parts as they move.
- **Joining:** with a gear selected, double-clicking a gear it meshes with at 90° moves it to the free spot nearest the click (also to move it round its partner). The context bar's Join button is Auto Join for a gear with such a partner within 40 mm (and nothing lined up to mate): a tap moves it to the nearest spot. When every spot is blocked, a message says there is no room.
- **Audit:** 90° pairs are listed with the meshing pairs (`kinematics.meshedGearPairs`, with their kind) and drive wheels through them; a 90° pair within 3 mm of mesh range but outside it is reported in `kinematics.nearMeshPairs` with how far off it is.
- **Play mode:** bevel and crown pairs turn each other by their tooth counts, each the way that keeps the teeth together where they meet (the ratio is read off the meshing rule itself, so it always agrees with how the gears were placed). A worm turns its gear one tooth per turn and only that way: a motor reaching the worm from the gear's side stalls and is listed ("… can't turn Worm Gear #1: a worm can only be turned from its own shaft"), and a load on the gear (an arm's weight) can't turn the gear or the worm.
- **Assistant:** `mesh_right_angle` lists a gear's mesh spots round a partner (`spot`, `axis`, `center`, `blocked`, `blockedBy`) without changing anything, or with `spot` puts the gear there, meshed, as one undo step (an `instanceId` to move or a `part` to add). Two parts that can't mesh at 90° are refused with the pairs that can.

### Fix assembly (Tools menu)

- **Tools → Fix assembly** (toolbar, next to Select; the same menu holds Chain builder and Track builder) lines up two blocks of parts that are nearly but not quite mated: pins a few mm off their holes, tilted a few degrees, pressed a little into each other, or sitting just short. Select either side (the male or the female part) and choose it; greyed out with nothing selected or while previewing history.
- **Blocks:** the parts joined to each other by pins, pegs and shafts seated in holes, plus the other members of any group they are in. Gears that only mesh and parts that only touch don't join blocks, so a claw resting against its arm, or a motor hole across a gear train, can be lined up. Near the selected part (its box grown by 20 mm) it pairs pins, pegs and shafts with holes they fit, and holes with holes a shaft fits, between the selected part's block and another block: axes within 20°, lines within 4.5 mm, within 4 mm along the line or overlapping. The block with the most pairs is the partner. When no other block is near, it looks inside the selected part's own block (the part and what hangs only off it, against the rest), so a part held by one joint with its other pins off can be fixed too.
- **Lining up** (`src/engine/fixAssembly.js`): the block with fewer parts moves (the selected one on a tie; never a block with a locked part) as one rigid piece. It is turned and moved, at any angle, so every pair shares a line (point-to-line fitting with Horn's method); pairs that disagree with the rest are dropped worst first until all are within 0.2 mm. A fit within 1.5° of square with its partner is made square when the pins still fit, so uneven CAD holes don't leave hundredths of a degree. More than 25° or 10 mm is refused ("Move the parts closer first").
- **Seating:** when the pairs are parallel, the block then slides along them: out of any overlap to the nearest clear spot (within 4 mm), or in until it touches the other block, and settles where the touching parts sit a whole number of quarter pitches apart (the collision meshes are shrunk, so contact is seen a little deep). A part already touching isn't pushed further.
- **Never makes a new overlap if it can help it:** after working out the smaller block's move, it checks the result against every other part. If that would make parts overlap that didn't before, it tries moving the other side instead: the parts held rigidly together (two or more pins, pegs or shafts, the bodies Play mode works out) with each part the pairs lined up with, such as a claw rather than the whole robot it hangs on. It keeps whichever move leaves fewer new overlaps, and says so: "… moved Claw 3x3 Beam R and 6 more parts onto Claw Crank Arm R (moving Claw Crank Arm R would have overlapped Claw Idler Shaft)". Never a piece with a locked part, a meshed gear or only part of a group, and never one whose move would change how deep a pin or peg joining it to the rest of its block is seated. It adds about a second on a build the size of the Clawbot, only when the first move would overlap. (The Clawbot's right claw, which only rests against its crank arm, is now a block of its own and moves onto the arm straight away: the arm and its shaft stay put and the claw moves 0.3 mm.)
- **A meshed gear never moves** (openspec precise-placement): when the block that would move holds a gear meshed with one outside it (as Play mode links them, 90° pairs included), it stays. Instead the other block's rigid pieces (as above) or, in either block, parts riding on a shaft through a round hole are tried; those on a shaft only slide along it and turn about it, the shaft staying put. The one with the fewest new overlaps, then the least turn, then the fewest parts moves, and the message says "(moving … would have taken Worm out of mesh with 36 Tooth Gear)". When none works, nothing moves: "Pin #4 is 0.58 mm and 0.9° out of line with Smart Motor #2. Nothing can move to line them up without taking Worm out of mesh with Gear 36."
- **Smart Motors sit square on their shafts:** the motor body stays where it is; before lining anything up, the shaft in a motor near the part whose socket is within 3° and 1 mm of it, with everything fixed on the shaft by square holes, turns about the socket onto its line, keeping its own turn and the hub angle: "Turned Worm shaft 0.9° to sit square in Smart Motor #2". It is left as it is, and the message says why, when the shaft or a part on it is locked, also sits in something else (a beam hole), or carries a gear meshed with one off the shaft: "Smart Motor #2 is 0.9° off Worm shaft; turning the shaft would take Worm out of mesh with Gear 36". Placing a motor does the same (placement step). The audit lists a motor more than 0.2° off its shaft.
- **In the 90° gear rig** (Pin #4 in a 1x1 corner connector on a 2x9 Beam riding the 36 Tooth Gear's shaft, 0.6 mm and 0.9° off a hole of the motor driving the worm), Fix assembly slides the beam, connector and pin together 0.74 mm along the gear's shaft. The gear, both shafts, the worm and the motor stay where they are (the worm is meshed, so its shaft isn't turned into the tilted motor), and the pin ends in both holes. A piece split off a block keeps every joint seated, so a motor never leaves the shaft in its socket.
- **Gear trains are laid out again** (openspec precise-placement): putting a shaft into a gear (or a gear on), or Fix assembly on a gear or a shaft carrying one when there is nothing else to line up, lays its train of spur gears out again from its start (the gear on a Smart Motor's shaft, else one on a shaft held by a beam). Going outward along the meshes, each gear free to move (only gears, shafts, collars and spacers, not locked, not held by a beam, pin or motor) moves sideways onto the beam or plate hole within 1.5 mm that gives the meshing distance from the gear before it, then turns with its shaft to mesh; gears on held shafts only turn. Beams, plates and motors never move, and a layout that would make an overlap isn't made. "Moved 36 Tooth Gear #3 0.48 mm onto the hole of 2x20 Beam #1". On a staggered 2x20 Beam a 24 and a 36 Tooth Gear sit on holes 32.39 mm apart (ideal 31.75 mm): meshed, 0.64 mm loose.
- **After a pin goes in** (double-click, Join, hole picking, the marked target, a copy), a hole on another block that the part's pins, pegs or shafts nearly reach (within 1 mm and 3°, up to 4 mm along the line) is lined up by the same rules, in the same history step as the insert (one undo takes back both); the message adds what moved, or what's out of line when nothing may move. Dragging doesn't do this (the release fit does its own). Engine action `line_up_after_insert`.
- **One step:** "Fix assembly: <moved part or group> onto <other part>", with the moved parts' overlap flags; the message says how many pins and holes were lined up. Nothing to do gives "No pins or holes near … are nearly lined up with another part" or "… is already lined up and seated". It runs in the engine (`fix_assembly` action), so it works the same on the dev server and the static site.

### Chain and track builder (Tools menu)

- **Tools menu → Build** (toolbar, next to Select): **Chain builder** and **Track builder…**, available when the selection holds two or more sprockets (8/16/24/32/40 Tooth) with parallel axles (within 2°) and their teeth in one plane (within 1 mm). Selected standoffs (`fastenerType === 'standoff'`) whose axles are parallel and whose body spans across the chain's plane act as smooth round guides (links rest on their 6.35 mm body at any angle, without turning them), though standoffs don't count toward the two sprockets. Any other selected part is an **obstacle** (see below). Otherwise both are greyed out and the menu says what is missing ("Chain and track fit sprockets, not gears", "…not in one plane", "…doesn't reach the chain", "…overlap").
- **Chain builder** wraps a loop of Chain Links round the sprockets and standoffs (`src/utils/chainPath.js`): one tooth per link round each sprocket (pivots on its pitch circle, 6.35 mm apart), resting on each standoff's body, and straight runs between them. Sprockets of different sizes are fine. When the selection has **enclosed** parts (inside the outer loop, between its top and return runs) or **crossing** sprockets (on the outer loop, but a straight run between their neighbours would pass through them), a dialog (`ChainRouteDialog`) lists each with two choices, **Under** and **Over** (for an enclosed part the top run dips under it or the return run rises over it; for a crossing sprocket one of them is the outside wrap, as the row's hint says, and the other passes the run the other side of it; no default, remembered per part for the page). Standoffs are asked about only when enclosed. The dialog highlights the focused/hovered part in the 3D view (`attentionIds`, pulsing in the hole-highlight colour without changing the selection). Impossible routes (parts too close for the chain to pass between, an outer part losing the chain, runs touching, passing through an unwrapped selected part, or a self-crossing loop) are refused by name.
- **A sprocket on both runs** (openspec chain-routing-around-parts): the outer loop is worked out by wrapping round all the parts, so a sprocket that sticks out past both runs (a big motor sprocket between two small ones in a line, as on the 2026-27 Herobot Flop's 8T–16T–8T drive) is wrapped by the top run over it and the bottom run under it, without asking. A run bent round an enclosed part or an obstacle that comes into contact with a selected sprocket wraps that sprocket there too. Either way the sprocket is turned once so its teeth sit in the chain on both runs: the run onto its second wrap lands on a tooth gap of the first, the chain leaving the part before a tooth sooner or later so a whole number of links reaches it. The message says "16 Tooth Sprocket on both runs" and `wrapped` lists it with side `both`. There is no "Both" choice: a tight chain can't touch a part between its runs on both of them.
- **Obstacles:** a selected part that isn't a sprocket or standoff (a beam, bracket, connector) is sliced where it crosses the chain's plane, across a link's width; the outline (convex, at most 12 corners, `src/utils/chainObstacles.js`) is a shape the chain bends round, its corners like standoffs of no width. One that a run would pass through gets a row in the dialog ("In a run's way; Over goes round its outside", highlighted like the others); Over takes the chain above it, Under below it. Links rest on its corners, pressing at most 0.5 mm into it (a tight contact, as VEX's own Flop chain does under its 2x20 Beam). A run that only meets one once bent by the other answers (riding over Flop's chain guide lifts the bottom run into a corner connector) asks about it then, in a new row. One clear of every run is left alone ("Not in the chain's way"), and one that doesn't reach the plane is left out by name. Tracks don't bend round obstacles: one in a run's way is left out.
- **Parts in the way:** before building, every planned link is checked against the parts that aren't selected (overlaps, not tight contacts). The Build menu hover text ("In the way: 2x20 Beam; select it to bend the chain round it"), the dialog (for the answers given) and the built chain's message name them; the chain is still built. The app gets the plan from the engine (`chain_plan` action), since slicing needs the parts' meshes.
- **Track builder** asks for an **Attachment** (Traction Link, Short, Medium or Long Intake Flap, or None) and, unless None, an **Every** slider from 1 to 20 tread links (both remembered while the page is open; Traction Link on every link by default), then builds the outer loop of Tank Tread Attachment Links with the attachment plugged into the two holes of every Nth tread link (links 1, N+1, 2N+1, …), facing out; when the count doesn't divide evenly the message says the last gap is shorter. Attachments count as held by their links, aren't collisions with them, and come apart with the track in Explode. A flap counts as on its tread link plugged either way round: a tread link looks the same turned half a turn about its own Y axis, so a flap on the other side of the link's pivot fits too (half of Flop's intake flaps are placed that way). Runs land on each sprocket where the chain turns in by no more than a tooth, never backwards, so outward attachments don't squeeze together. Tracks only go round the outside of sprockets and standoffs; any selected part inside the loop is left out and named ("tracks only go round the outside").
- **Fit:** a chain can't stretch, so the link count is the fewest that reach all the way round: never more than 0.75 mm short of the path (the play in its joints), at most a link longer (VEX IQ links are all alike, so odd counts are fine). Links stay 6.35 mm apart (eased at most 0.2 mm). The other runs are straight and tight; the spare length hangs on one run between two outer parts in a smooth curve sagging toward the floor (a catenary; out of the loop on the longest run when the sprockets lie flat, and on the longest run when every run touches an enclosed part). When the hanging run would droop 1 mm or more below its straight line and the sprockets stand up, the builder asks **Slack on: Lower run / Upper run** (remembered while the page is open): the lower run hangs below the sprockets, the upper run hangs down into the loop, leaving and meeting its sprockets a tooth or two later as a real chain does, never into their teeth. Chain builder builds at once when there's nothing to ask. No link turns more than a tooth step where a run meets a sprocket, so there are no kinks. The message gives the gap ("The chain is 4.6 mm longer than the path") and which sprocket to move how far for an exact fit, and says it will sag when that's more than half a link. Each sprocket turns (with its shaft group) so a tooth gap sits at a chain pivot; standoffs aren't turned.
- **One step, one group:** the links and grippers become a new group "Chain N" / "Track N" (selected; the sprockets and standoffs stay outside it). Building, grouping and the sprocket turns are one history step labeled e.g. "Chain builder: 52 Chain Links round 3 sprockets and 1 standoff (under 8 Tooth Sprocket #3)"; one undo takes it all back. The chain doesn't follow a sprocket moved later: delete the group and build again.
- **Overlaps and audit** (`src/utils/chainFit.js`): neighbouring links, links on the sprockets or standoffs they wrap (from inside or outside the loop, and the links leaving or reaching one) and grippers in their links aren't collisions. The audit joins links to each other, to their sprockets and grippers to their links, so a chain on a sprocket counts as held and joins its sprockets into one piece. A chain that bends with no sprocket or standoff holding that bend (a tooth's angle or sharper; e.g. after a sprocket or guide was moved) has its links counted as unsecured and listed in `chainBends`.
- **Part data:** sprockets carry measured `gearSpecs` (pitch radius for 6.35 mm chain, tooth and gap angle, tooth band); Chain Link, Tank Tread Attachment Link and Traction Link carry `chainSpecs` (pivots, gripper holes, the gripper's seat in a tread link).

### Bend sheet and shaped parts

- **Shaped parts** (openspec shaped-parts-and-sheet-bending; `src/utils/shapedParts.js`): a part copy can carry its own shape, `shape: { kind, ... }`, saved with the build, in starters (an optional eighth column), library builds and history (its own step, e.g. "Bend …", undoable), copied by Duplicate, Clone and Ctrl+double-click, and flipped by Mirror. Everything that uses a part's solid (the 3D view, overlap and tight-fit checks, holes, pin seating, the audit, explode, screenshots, the MCP server) uses the copy's shaped part: the catalog part with its own id (`<partId>~<key>`), holes and box, its mesh made from the catalog model by the kind. A copy with no shape is exactly the catalog part. The first kind is a bent plastic sheet; wires, rubber bands and ropes can be added later as kinds of their own without touching any of that.
- **How a sheet bends** (`src/utils/sheetBend.js`): a 2x12, 6x6 or 6x12 Plastic Sheet bends along lines parallel to one set of its hole rows, never twisting. It stays flat through the rows that were pinned before it was bent (its held rows); between each anchored row and the one before it, it follows one smooth curve (the least-bending cubic, or, when that would bend one way and then back because the sheet has spare length, a straight run, one circular arc no tighter than 3 mm radius, and a straight run). When the holes were picked and no such bend clears the other parts, a longer search (about a second or more) tries S-bends and loops: paths of the sheet's exact length, no tighter than 3 mm radius, never touching themselves, forced through a grid of waypoints, gentlest first, and the first that clears the parts is offered with a note saying it goes one way and then back; it is saved as the bend's variant number and rebuilt the same way that leaves each row in line with the sheet there and is exactly as long as the sheet between the two rows (12.7 mm a row), with the least bending that allows and nowhere tighter than 3 mm radius; past the last anchored row it runs straight to its edge. It keeps its thickness and length, except that a row up to 1 mm out of reach is anchored with the sheet stretched that much between the two rows, so its pins seat (shown with the choice and kept with the bend). Its holes follow the sheet, each pointing straight through it. The mesh is cut into 1 mm strips across the bend before bending, so it curves instead of folding at corners.
- **Tools → Bend sheet…** (with a plastic sheet and one other part selected; a bent sheet alone offers Flatten) opens a dialog over a light backdrop listing every way a later row of the sheet can be anchored to that part's holes, e.g. "sheet row 4 → 1x12 Beam, upper face": a line of the part's holes parallel to the sheet's rows (within 3°), the sheet's holes in that row lining up with them along the row (within 1 mm), on a face of the part (each face is its own choice; a beam's face is its raised rails), within reach. Pointing at a choice shows the sheet bent that way in the 3D view and rings the target holes; Apply bends it as one step, moving the pins in its moving rows with it. **By holes** (openspec bend-sheet-by-holes): with only an unbent sheet selected, or a sheet hole set as the Alt+click target, Tools → Bend sheet starts hole picking; the hole under the cursor lights without Alt and a plain click picks it. Click one of the sheet's holes (marked in its own colour; its row is the row anchored, never another), then a hole on any other part. The sheet is shown bent so the marked hole lands on that hole, with the fit that lines up the most holes of the row first; the banner names the face ("Fit 1 of 2: 4 holes line up on 2x10 Beam, upper face"); the mouse wheel, `R` and `Shift+R` step through the other fits, Enter or Keep makes it one history step, `Esc` or Cancel drops it (a second `Esc` or Stop ends picking), and clicking another hole picks again. A marked hole in a row already held or anchored, or not within 1 mm of the picked hole along the row, is refused with the reason. Error messages over the 3D view stay until closed with ×. Use it again with another part to anchor a further row; only rows past the last anchored one are offered, and the sheet up to that row keeps its shape. Only the sheet up to the row being anchored has to be clear of other parts: what the rest of it still runs into is noted ("anchor a later row to bend it clear"). With no choices the dialog says why: no row pinned yet, the holes aren't parallel (it would twist), out of reach by N mm, too tight a bend, or it would pass through a part. The Inspector shows a bent sheet's bend under **Shape**.
- **The printed lattice:** clear sheets show the honeycomb pattern with a ring round each hole, as VEX's model does, in light grey on the face VEX prints it on, bent with the sheet; their outline and hole rims are drawn light grey too. The pattern comes from VEX's own Flop model (its flat 6x12 and 6x6 bodies' edge lines) by `node scripts/extract-sheet-patterns.mjs`, into `public/sheet-patterns.json` in each sheet's own frame (hole rings within 0.04 mm of the library's holes). The 2x12 has no source yet, so it shows plain.
- **Stacked sheets:** where another sheet already lies on that face at those holes, the sheet goes on top of it, one sheet thickness plus 0.1 mm out, so both pin through the same holes.
- **Sheets flex:** a plastic sheet pressing into another part counts as a tight fit, not an overlap, when it would have to flex at most 1.5 mm along its own normal to clear it (VEX's own bent sheets press 1.3–1.4 mm into the ends of Flop's side plates); two sheets pinned through the same holes count their contact as a tight fit. The app, the MCP engine, the audit and Bend sheet use the same rule (`src/utils/sheetContact.js`).
- **Tested on Flop:** its 6x12 intake sheet is held flat on two rows on corner connectors and bent onto a 1x12 Beam (row 4) and a 1x6 Beam (row 6, stretched 0.72 mm); its 6x6 hangs upright on two rows and curls onto the same 1x6 Beam on top of the 6x12. Both lie within about 0.5 mm on average of VEX's own bent sheets, which are themselves stretched up to 1.7 mm between rows.

### Rubber bands and belts (Tools → Band builder)

openspec rubber-bands; `src/utils/bandPath.js` (spots, path, stretch), `src/utils/bandRoute.js` (stringing a band on a build, following parts), `src/utils/bandShape.js` (the `band` shape kind), `src/engine/bandRouting.js` (`place_band`), `src/engine/bandStrand.js` (the way from one hook to the next), `src/engine/bandHull.js` (parts' outer corners), `src/engine/bandFit.js` (the band overlap rule), `src/components/useBandBuilder.js` and `BandBuilderPanel.jsx` (the builder).

- **Band parts:** Silicone Rubber Band #32, #64, Thin #64 and #117B, the 2x30 mm Square Silicone Belt and the 2x30/40/50/60 mm Rubber Belts. Each one's free length, width and thickness are measured from its own model (#117B: 354 mm round, 6.3 wide, 1.5 thick; the 2x30 belt: 94 mm). A band placed from the library is the flat ring it always was; a strung band is a copy with a `band` shape (its route and the path it takes), drawn as a flat strip its width and thickness along the path.
- **Spots** are single features a band can go on, found from part data: a standoff's body (`post:body`), a pin's leg (`post:pin`, 2.1 mm radius), a shaft end (`post:shaft`), a pulley's groove (`groove`), a gear's or sprocket's rim (`rim`), one tooth gap the band passes through (`gap:<k>`) or is hooked in (`gaphook:<k>`), numbered round the gear from its own tooth phase so the number turns with it, a beam's or bracket's rounded end or outer corner round a hole (`edge:<holePortId>`), a straight beam's or plate's long sides and ends round its thickness (`edge:side+|side-|end+|end-`), and hand entries in `src/utils/bandSpotTable.js` (the Rubber Band Anchor's hook, an estimate).
- **The path** is the shortest loop that meets the route: straight runs between spots, curving round each circle on its side (outside: round the far side from the loop's middle; inside: the band bends the other way), through each gap, each post at the height along it that shortens the loop most within its free stretch (outside other parts' holes on it, beside other bands). `wraps` (1–3) add turns side by side; `cross` makes the run to the next spot a figure-8. A route can name a spot twice (a thin band folded out and back round a corner): a pass at the same height lies one band thickness further out. On a post, the band's middle stays half its width in from the ends of the free stretch, so it doesn't sink into the part the post stands on.
- **Hooks and guides:** the first and last spots, and posts, pins, grooves, gaps and the anchor between them, are hooks (the band is looped on them); edges are guides (it bends round them on the way). A band on two hooks goes **there and back**: out from one hook to the other and back over the same bends, side by side. Three or more hooks make a **loop**. Either can be forced (`back`).
- **The way round:** each stretch from one hook to the next takes the shortest clear way round the parts between, as a tight string would, bending only on the outer corners of their real shapes (the sharp edges of each part's convex hull; a part with none, like a standoff in the way, uses its box), through any edges clicked in that stretch. A bend is a route entry that carries its own corner (`feature: "wrap"`, `line`, `wrap`), so it follows the part. Up to three other ways (over the parts, or under them) can be stepped through. A run through a gear's or sprocket's web goes through the gap that keeps the band shortest. Known limit: a hull fills notches, so a band never bends round an inside corner by itself; click a spot there.
- **Stretch:** path length ÷ free length; slack (grey) under 1×, snug (green) to 2×, tight (amber) to 3×, too far (red) past it, and the pull in newtons. These limits and each size's stiffness are assumed values in `src/sim/bandStrength.js`. The size closest to 1.5× is suggested when a band is slack or too far. A band that would be slack (longer than its route) isn't placed: the message names the size that fits (`allowSlack` places it anyway; imports aren't refused). One already placed that goes slack as parts move stays, shown slack.
- **Tools → Band builder:** the size is Choose for me (the size nearest 1.5× on the route) unless one is picked; click spots in the view in order (by touch, tap a spot to light it and tap it again to add it; the context bar's wheel steps to the next spot near it); the spot under the pointer lights (a ring, or a bead in a gap, with its name in the panel), and R, Shift+R or the wheel step to the next spot near the pointer. A see-through band coloured by stretch follows the pointer. After each click the see-through band shows the way the band will really take, bends included. With three or more spots the panel shows whether it goes **There and back** or as a **Loop** and lets either be forced (from one pin to another under two beams: click the pin, the beams' bottom edges, the other pin, Done). Clicking the first spot again, Enter or Done closes the band (one history step, e.g. "Band: #117B round 2 spots (…)"); Backspace takes off the last spot; Esc cancels. The panel lists the route with Outside/Inside, wraps and Cross for each spot, controls for the lit spot, the stretch read-out (with a button for the suggested size), **Other way round** (the next of up to four ways, e.g. under the beams instead of over; R does the same while no spot is lit), the over/under question when no way is found, and a ring close-up of each gear, sprocket or pulley on the route: numbered notches and the posts on it as dots, with the route drawn over them; clicking one adds or removes it. Parts bent round are listed in the message; over/under is asked only when no clear way is found. A post takes a band only where at least 1.5 mm of it is free of the parts it goes into, measured on their shapes (`postCover` in the engine); a pin buried in the parts it joins is refused with a message.
- **Band between these / Belt round these** (Tools) start the builder with the nearest spots of two selected parts, or round the outside of selected pulleys, hubs or sprockets on parallel axles.
- **Editing:** the Inspector shows a strung band's size (changeable), length, stretch and pull, and each spot with Outside/Inside, wraps and Cross; **Edit route** shows a handle on each spot (drag it to another spot, or off every spot to drop it) and one in the middle of each run (drag it onto a gear's rim: it hops gap to gap and puts the run through the gap). **Flatten** (also Tools → Flatten band) takes the band off its spots. Move and Rotate are greyed out for a strung band.
- **Bands follow their parts:** when a part a band is on moves, turns, is hidden or removed (in the app, over MCP, or by an action), the band is strung again in the same history step, and a band the user strung is routed again from their own clicks (`shape.picks`) when it now overlaps a part or a shorter way has opened; with fewer than two spots left it goes back to a flat ring and the message says so. Duplicate and Clone carry a band with its parts (a band copied alone is a flat ring); Mirror strings it on the mirrored parts, through the mirrored gears' matching gaps. **One overlap rule** (`bandOverlaps`): a band overlaps a part when the middle sheet of its strip (sampled every 1 mm across its width) sits more than 0.5 mm inside that part, its own parts included, except where it curves round its own spot (and a band width either side) or passes a gear's tooth gap; other bands don't count. The engine sets a band's overlap flag (placing, following, saving, and when the app loads a build); the 3D view uses that flag and doesn't test bands itself, so a band lying on a beam isn't red. A wide band that has no room beside the bands already on a post is refused, saying so. The audit leaves strung bands out of its fastening rules.
- **Checked on Hero Bot Swish** (`node scripts/check-bands.mjs`, `scripts/fixtures/swish-bands.json` from `scripts/make-band-fixture.mjs`): the six bands VEX's CAD shows are read and solved against VEX's meshes. The intake bands each wrap four standoffs and pass gaps 17 and 23 (or 5 and 11) of both inner sprockets, within 1.5 mm of VEX's band. The arm bands run from the arm standoff under a chassis beam to the chassis pin and back, and the latch belts are folded from a standoff round the right-angle beam's ends to the pawl's pin.

### Play mode (Tools menu)

- **Bands in Play mode:** a strung band is no body: each frame its path is found again from its spots carried by their bodies, its strip is redrawn in its stretch colour, and leaving Play puts it back. With physics, it pulls each body holding its spots along its path, harder the more it stretches (assumed stiffness); a band whose spots are all on one body pulls on nothing. Without physics, bands don't pull and the panel says so. On Swish, the arm bands pull the catapult arm toward the chassis when nothing holds it.
- **Tools → Play mode** (openspec play-mode; ticked while on, `Esc` or the panel's × leaves it) runs the Smart Motors and shows how the build moves. While it's on nothing can be selected or edited and no history steps are made; leaving puts every part back where it is in the build. The view keys (orbit, `P`, `1`–`3`) still work. Explode is off while playing.
- **The Play panel** lists every Smart Motor that drives a shaft, each with a throttle from full reverse through stop to full forward (up to 120 rpm either way), its speed, and **Stalled** in red when it can't turn. A throttle springs back to stop when let go, unless that motor's **Hold** checkbox is ticked (double-click a throttle to stop it). Pointing at a row lights its motor up in the view; stalled motors stay lit. Below the motors are global toggles for **Limit speed by load** and **Simulate gravity**. Below that, "N parts don't move as built" opens the list of what Play mode couldn't set up.
- **How the parts move** (`src/sim/bodyGraph.js`, worked out by the engine's `play_setup` action): parts joined on two or more separate lines (pins, pegs, standoffs, shafts) move as one body; a body held on by one line turns on it as a hinge. A square shaft turns everything whose square bore fits it (within 2° of its flats); a round bore, or a square more than 2° off, turns freely on it and is listed ("doesn't fit the square shaft"). A Smart Motor's hub takes the shaft at any angle. Meshing gears turn each other the opposite way by their tooth ratio, a gear moves a rack along its teeth, and sprockets on one chain or track turn each other the same way by their ratio. Chain and track links have no body of their own: each track rides with the body holding its sprockets (the one most of its sprockets hang on within two hinges: Flop's intake tracks ride the intake frame as the tower lifts it, its drive chains the chassis). A track on a single sprocket, like the intake's two tracks of 8 tread links, also runs round its loop as the sprocket turns, one link a tooth, each link moving to where the next one was (blended between the two), its flaps and grippers with it; tracks on several sprockets are only tied by ratio and their links don't run round. Gears 1–3 mm out of mesh are listed and don't drive each other (nor rub). A pin a little off a hole's line (a diagonal brace, within 0.75 mm) counts as in it only when at least 1 mm of it is inside, so a pin tip grazing a beam hole doesn't lock a gear to the beam.
- **What holds still:** with no floor or gravity, the largest piece of the robot, and any piece with a driving motor, holds one body still (the one carrying most driving motors, else the largest). A piece not joined to those stays still, blocks nothing and is listed.
- **Simulate gravity & floor:** when **Simulate gravity** is on, unanchored pieces fall under gravity onto a ground floor collider positioned right beneath the robot. Bodies pinned on a single line that aren't motor-driven droop under gravity. Gravitational weight opposes mechanisms lifting upward and assists downward motion. Battery bays latch seated batteries (Gen 1 and Gen 2) so they don't fall out of their brains.
- **Limit speed by load:** when enabled, motor speeds drop realistically above 1.4 W mechanical power according to $(1.4 / \tau_\text{load}) \times 9.55\text{ rpm}$, tapering smoothly down to 0 at the motor's stall torque of 0.414 N·m. Mechanical load calculates gravitational torque along the kinematic chain, bearing friction, and for drivetrains, rolling load from the total robot weight.
- **Collisions & impact wobble** (`src/sim/physics.js`, Rapier, loaded only when Play mode opens): every part collides as a convex hull of its mesh, so parts stop when they run into each other. When an arm or mechanism hits a stop or another body, it settles with an inertia-scaled elastic wobble that naturally damps according to the moving assembly's mass, rather than jarringly freezing. Connector pins have no shape (they sit inside holes) but standoffs do (solid posts that stick out), and the tread links and flaps of a track on a moving frame (the intake's) collide as part of that frame. Loose pieces hold still and block nothing unless gravity is simulated.
- **A motor stops when what it moves hits something:** each motor knows everything it moves (its shaft, its gear train and the linkage hanging off it). When any of that presses into another part and is still going deeper in the commanded direction, the motor stops, its whole train with it, and shows **Stalled**; only the opposite throttle is allowed until it frees. If the physics can't start, the panel says "no collisions" and parts turn by gear ratio alone (`src/sim/kinematic.js`).
- **Drop test** (openspec drop-load-network): the Play panel's **Drop test** section picks a height (`0.15 m` to `1.5 m`) and a landing (**Flat**, **Front**, **Back**, **Left side**, **Right side**, **Corner**; +X is the front, as on the view cube), then **Drop** and **Reset**. Rapier turns the robot so that face is down, lifts it and drops it; at its first floor contact `src/sim/impact.js` works out the hit for the whole robot at once:
  - **Holding strengths** come from one table, `src/sim/fastenerStrength.js`, every value marked *assumed* until measured (the panel says "Strengths are estimates" until then). A pin end grips each part by a snap ridge (the part at its tip only) plus friction for every mm it's seated, so deeper seating holds more. Two parts on a pin come apart only when the parts on one side slide off their end of the pin; a shoulder, cap or standoff body in the way blocks that way (measured from each pin's mesh as `fastenerSpecs.blockersZ`), so a capped pin's capped side never lets go. Rubber collars hold a few newtons by friction; spacers and washers only push; a square shaft slides freely in its square hole, and nothing on a shaft can leave it sideways (only by sliding off its end); a tire on its hub and a Gen 1 battery in its Brain are one piece.
  - **The robot as a network:** every part is a node (moving and turning); beams, plates and shafts flex as chains or grids of nodes, and connectors flex between their groups of pegs and holes. Joints are springs with a little play (pins rattle 0.15 mm, then bear stiffly); parts pinned face to face bear on each other across the face they share and grip by friction while pressed; meshed gears and chains hold their ratio; a Smart Motor holds its shaft up to its stall torque. The floor pushes (never pulls), softly on tires. The ~30–70 ms of the hit is stepped every 12 µs, until no joint's load is still rising.
  - **Failure:** each joint's pull (tension only), sideways load and prying (through the face edge its part pivots on) are set against its strength. A pin or peg pulled past its grip slides out, gripping less as it goes, and lets go only once it has slid its whole seated depth; a Rubber Shaft Collar slides gripping all the way and comes off only after sliding the shaft inside it and any shaft past it. A brief pull just loosens them (the report lists them as loosened, with how far they slid); a harder pull pushes the parts apart faster, so it comes out sooner. A joint pushed sideways past its strength lets go at once. Load passes to the joints still holding during the same hit. Pieces left with no joints to their body split off and fly with the speed the impact gave them, or swing on the one pin line left. The result depends only on the build, height and landing (moving or mirroring the build changes nothing).
  - **Report:** joints that let go, each with how (pulled out, pried out, slid off, sheared) and its load against its strength; parts that came off; weak spots (joints that used 70% or more of their strength); the robot's peak deceleration in g and its busiest joint in N. Hovering one highlights it. **Reset** (or closing Play mode) mends everything without touching the build or undo history.
  - **After the hit** the robot leaves the floor with the bounce, rocking and arm swing the impact worked out; pieces that broke off leave with their share of it (no faster than half the landing speed, spinning no faster than 25 rad/s, since light parts are still ringing when the impact ends). During a drop Rapier steps every 1 ms with continuous collision on, so nothing passes through the floor. Pieces that broke away collide again with the parts they left once half a millimetre separates them (or once pressed half a millimetre deeper), so a loose Brain stops against the plates it sat on; Rapier takes 12 passes per step during a drop so loose pieces don't sink into each other; **Slow motion** plays the drop 2, 4 or 8 times slower than real time. **Highlight loose parts** lights up the parts a drop knocked off and the ones left hanging by a single pin (the report lists those as "Hanging by one pin").
  - Rapier's part masses come from the same table (Brain 0.18 kg, battery 0.14 kg, Smart Motor 0.082 kg, other parts from their box and ABS).
- **Checked on the starters** (`node scripts/check-play-mode.mjs`): the Clawbot's drive motors reach 120 rpm, its arm swings by its 12:36 gears (ratio held within 1%) until it folds onto the tower and stalls, and reversing frees it; Clawbot and Flop stay still at rest; each drop landing puts its face lowest, a `1.5 m` Clawbot drop breaks joints and splits pieces off, and Reset mends everything. `node scripts/check-impact.mjs` checks the strength rules and the impact on hand-made builds (deeper seating holds more, a capped side can't let go, a heavier load pries harder, bending softens the hit, bracing protects an arm, load passes to the joint that's left, collars slide only on side landings, moving or mirroring a build changes nothing, a higher drop never does less) and that a Flop drop is worked out in under 1 s.

3. **🎯 Snap & Mate to Port (`mate_parts`):**
   - Declaratively snaps any source port on the selected part (or `Auto` primary bore/socket/hole) to any target port on another placed part.

   - Builds a complete, phase-synchronized gear train along any selected structural beam starting at a chosen `pin_hole` port.
   - Presets include `3 × 36T (1:1)`, `5 × 36T (Full Side)`, `2 × 36T`, `12T → 36T (1:3 Torque)`, and `60T → 36T (1.67:1 Speed)`.
   - Automatically alternates gear roll angles (`180° / teeth`) to prevent tooth-on-tooth clashes, centers gears between inner and outer rails, and optionally inserts `3x Pitch Shafts` (`228-2500-2221`).
5. **🪞 Symmetry Mirror (`mirror_subassembly`):**
   - Mirrors the selected part (or the entire half-assembly) across `Z = 0` (`Left ↔ Right`) or `X = 0` (`Front ↔ Rear`).
   - Each part is turned to its mirror image (shared with `place_parts` `mirror`): of the three turns that reflect the part through one of its own middle planes, the one whose pins and openings land on the true mirror image's wins, then the one whose shape matches best. Offset connectors, rubber collars, motors and capped shafts come out the right way round; parts whose surface pattern or tread isn't symmetric get the closest turn with their holes in place, and parts with no exact mirror are flagged `mirrorApproximate`.

6. **CAD & Semantic Metadata Readout:**

## 4. 6-Stage Mechanical & Kinematic Audit (panel) + 3-View Blueprint (dialog)

**Tools → Audit** opens or closes the **Audit panel** (ticked while open); **Tools → Blueprint** shows the 3-view drawing on its own (a dialog with Open Full SVG). Also available via MCP tools `audit_assembly` / `capture_viewport_views`:

- **6-Stage Engineering Verification (`isMechanicallySound`):**
  1. **Solid Collisions (`collisionCount`):** Pairwise concave BVH mesh intersection check across all visible parts. Tight fits are listed apart (`tightFits`, `tightFitCount`; one entry per pair) and don't make the build unsound; the Audit panel shows them as notes.
  2. **Floating & Half-Seated Fasteners (`floatingFastenerCount`, `halfSeatedFastenerCount`):** A pin, peg or shaft only counts as joining parts when it actually **sits inside a matching hole** on each of them (same matching rules as the aligned-hole highlighting: axes within `2°` and `0.25 mm`, overlapping along the axis by more than `0.05 mm`). Parts whose boxes merely touch don't count. A fastener seated in no part is *floating*; seated on one side only, it's *half-seated*. Fasteners plugged into each other (a standoff in a Standoff Extender) count as one stack, which joins every part any of them is seated in; `joints` lists the other fastener with `fastener: true`. `joints` lists what every fastener sits in, and `pegJoints` lists parts joined by their own pegs (e.g. corner connectors).
  3. **Unsecured & Heavy Parts (`unsecuredPartCount`, `underSecuredHeavyPartCount`):** Checks that every non-fastener part has $\ge 1$ seated fastener or peg, and heavy components (Brain, Smart Motors, Battery) meet their `minFastenersRequired` threshold. Rubber Shaft Collars count as parts held on a shaft, not as shafts.
  4. **Connectivity Graph (`connectedComponentCount`, `disconnectedIslandCount`):** Builds the graph from seated joints, meshed gears and clamped parts to verify the robot forms a single unified assembly (`0` disconnected islands).
  5. **Shaft Protrusions (`protrudingShaftCount`):** Flags shafts reaching more than `4.5 mm` past the outer faces of the parts they sit in (collars included).
  - **Real hole depths:** hole depths are measured on each part's 3D model. A hole listed as going through but blocked inside is treated as a blind hole from each face that is open at least `1 mm` deep; for example, the Smart Motor's back face only has `~0.4 mm` marks, not holes.
  6. **Kinematic Drivetrain Trace (`kinematics`):** Traces torque transmission from each `Smart Motor` $\rightarrow$ `Motor Shaft` $\rightarrow$ `Driver Gear` $\rightarrow$ `Meshed Idler/Driven Gears` $\rightarrow$ `Driven Shafts` $\rightarrow$ `Driven Wheels`.
- **The Audit panel (`src/components/AuditPanel.jsx`, `src/utils/auditIssues.js`):**
  - It slides up from the bottom of the 3D view (up to 42% of its height) and **stays open** while the user works: the 3D view above it keeps working (select, orbit, move). The header says "● Mechanically sound (0 issues)" or "▲ N warnings"; a row of small counters (overlaps, loose pins, half-seated, unpinned, under-fastened, not joined, overhanging shafts, and the drivetrain "1M → 2G → 2/4W") sits under it. Refresh runs the audit again; × (or Tools → Audit again) closes it.
  - **It keeps itself up to date:** while open, the audit runs again (after a short pause) whenever the build changes, with no blueprint work. Nothing else in the app waits on it.

## 5a. Build History, Undo/Redo & Timeline

Shared history code: [`src/utils/buildHistory.js`](file:///Users/adriangonsalves/projects/vexiq_builder/src/utils/buildHistory.js). Timeline: [`src/components/HistoryTimeline.jsx`](file:///Users/adriangonsalves/projects/vexiq_builder/src/components/HistoryTimeline.jsx). Checks: `node scripts/check-history.mjs`.

- **Steps:**
  - Every change to the parts becomes one step with a label, a time and a source: `app`, `mcp`, `assistant` (in-page WebMCP) or `action` (smart actions).
  - Recorded changes: add, move, rotate, mate, delete, duplicate, recolor, hide/show, lock/unlock, group, ungroup, rename a group, clear, starter, and moving an overlapping part back. A change to a group or several selected parts (move, turn, hide, lock, delete, clone) is one step listing every part.
  - Not recorded: selection, the camera, explode view, viewport settings, Select mode, open Outliner folders, the locate pin, and the app's own overlap-flag updates.
  - Steps store only what changed, so long histories stay small.
- **Typing:** typing in an Inspector field, dragging the color picker, or repeated nudges on one part within 1 s merge into one step.
- **Undo / Redo:**
  - Unlimited, from the toolbar arrows or **Ctrl/Cmd+Z** and **Shift+Ctrl/Cmd+Z** / **Ctrl+Y**.
  - In text fields those keys keep their normal text editing.
  - After undo or redo, the moved parts' overlap status is checked again.
- **Linear or branches (Settings → History, advanced options):** by default history is linear: undoing and then making a change drops the undone steps, like most apps. With **Keep branches**, the undone steps stay as a branch and nothing is ever deleted. The choice applies to the app, its bridge and the assistant's MCP tools alike; branches already in a history stay switchable either way.
- **Timeline (History button, clock icon):**
  - A bar under the 3D view with one mark per step, colored by source (App blue, MCP amber, Assistant purple, Smart action green), plus "Step N of M" and hover tips with the label, source, time and part counts.
  - Click or drag to go to any step.
  - A step that touched many parts, such as one `place_parts` call, plays its parts one at a time while you scrub through it. The view is read-only part-way through a step, with a **Go to end of step** button.
  - **Play** (1×/2×/4×) replays the build from the start. Any edit, undo, redo or MCP change stops playback.
  - Branch markers open a menu to switch to another branch.
  - Whether the timeline is shown is remembered in this browser.
- **Sync:**
  - The app, the MCP server and smart actions all record into the same saved history.
  - A browser save built on an older history (an MCP step landed in between) is refused with `409`. The app then takes the saved build and puts its change on top, so neither side loses a step.
  - Each browser tab has its own id, so open tabs stay in step with each other.

## 5b. Static Site (no server)

- **Build it:** `npm run build:static` writes plain files to `dist-static/` for any static host (GitHub Pages, Netlify, Cloudflare Pages, S3). For a sub-folder, set the base path: `VEXIQ_BASE=/vexiq/ npm run build:static`. The site is about 105 MB, mostly `models/`. It holds only `catalog.json`, `part-metadata.json` and `models/` from `public/`, never the saved build, settings or blueprint.
- **What works:** everything you build with. The part library, placing and moving parts, every smart action (auto-fasten, mate, gear trains, chain and track builder with its over/under and slack questions, mirror, Select Connected, audit and blueprint), Explode, undo/redo and the timeline, and the build library.
- **How:** the assembly engine's core (`src/engine/core.js`, the same code the MCP server uses) runs in two Web Workers (`src/engine/worker.js`), one for actions and one for explode plans, so the page stays responsive. - **Hidden:** the MCP status in the title and the Assistant (MCP) section in Settings. There's no assistant, live sync between tabs or screenshot relay on the static site.

### Offline use and installing (static site only)

- **Settings → Offline → Use offline** (off by default) makes the site work with no internet. Turning it on shows the download size (about 105 MB) and how much space the browser has, and refuses if there isn't enough. **Download** saves the app, the catalog, part metadata, every part model, the icons and the web app manifest, with a progress bar ("41 of 105 MB"), **Cancel**, and **Resume**, which fetches only what isn't saved yet. A lost connection, a full device or a file that fails three times stops it with the reason.
- **Available offline:** shown in Settings and as a green label next to the part count once the copy is complete and current. Selecting the label opens the Offline section. Offline, the whole site works: library thumbnails, any part, every smart action, Explode, Audit with blueprint, saving, Load… and Export.
- **Keeping it:** the download asks the browser for persistent storage and says whether it agreed. The section always reminds that builds live only in this browser and that **File → Export** is the backup. If the browser clears the copy, the section says so and offers Resume.
- **Installing:** after the download (or from **Install the app**), steps for this browser: an **Install app** button in Chrome and Edge, Share → **Add to Home Screen** on iPad and iPhone, File → **Add to Dock** in Safari on a Mac, or a note that this browser can't install it. Installed, it opens in its own window. On iPad and iPhone a Home Screen app keeps its own storage, so in Safari the steps come first, with how to move builds across (Export, then Load… → Open from computer…) and **Download in Safari anyway**.
- **Installed without the download:** however the app was installed (Install app, the browser's own install button, Add to Home Screen), opening it as an installed app with Use offline off and a connection shows "Use VEX IQ without internet? Downloads the app and all parts (about 105 MB)." with **Download** (space check, then it downloads straight away with Settings open at Offline) and **Not now** (asked again next time it opens; after two Not nows it stops asking). The install steps say the app will offer this.
- **New versions:** while Use offline is on, the app checks for a newer published version on opening, on coming back online and on returning to it (at most once a minute; offline never counts). A newer one hides the label and shows "A new version of VEX IQ Builder is out. ## 5c. Importing VEX CAD (instructions.online)

- **`node scripts/import-cad.mjs <project id | instructions.online link | saved .json> [--clear] [--dry-run] [--save file] [--report file] [--lift mm]`** builds an official VEX CAD assembly (a Cadasio project, as shown on instructions.online) in the app through the MCP server, with the same checks and history as an agent: batches of 80 `place_parts` (structural parts before pins), one group per top subassembly, then the audit.
  - **The finished build:** the project is downloaded with its instruction steps, which are run up to the step named "Completed View" (the steps after it are exploded and showcase views). Parts hidden there are left out ("hidden by the instructions": on Flop, extra pins, two 2x12 Beams and the rubber-band anchors shown during the build). Poses still come from the CAD: the finished view nudges a few parts (Flop's slanted 1x18 Beams by 2.4 mm) without the pins in them, which would make overlaps the CAD doesn't have.
  - **Bent sheets:** a CAD sheet whose frame entry says `"bend": "pins"` (Flop's 228-7711-711 and -712) is laid flat on two neighbouring rows of the sheet pins that hold it on one part, and bent through each later row of pins within its reach onto the part those pins go into, as Bend sheet would; a later sheet stacks on an earlier one. The report lists each sheet's anchored rows and stretches, and rows it couldn't anchor. VEX's flat copies of bent sheets (other bodies of the same part, with no pins) are still left out.
  - **Kits:** a node carrying a part number the library lacks but made of library parts (Swish's "228-7980 VEX IQ Catapult Arm") is walked into, so its parts are placed, grouped by the kit's name ("Catapult Arm").
  - **Bands:** each rubber band or belt VEX's CAD shows stretched (visible in the finished view) is strung as a band: its mesh is split into bands (Swish's two intake bands are bodies of one part), the spots it lies against are found and ordered by which runs the band covers (a closed loop, or a thin loop folded out and back), spots it only passes are dropped, the order whose solved path best fits VEX's mesh is kept, and a run that would pass through a part goes round it on the side VEX's band does. Each is placed with `place_band` after the parts, and the report lists its spots and stretch; hidden stand-in bands are left out.
  - Every library part is converted with the frame table; cables, cable plugs, game pieces, hidden parts and part numbers the library lacks are left out and listed with the reason. Two copies of a part filling the same space (Flop's 2x12 Beams turned end for end) are placed once. Each part's label is `<subassembly>: <CAD instance>`, with the parent node added when it would repeat (two chains' `228-2500-230-29`). The build is raised so its lowest part rests on the floor.
  - The report gives parts placed, left out and why, duplicates, part types placed by an unchecked rule, and the audit summary.
- **The CAD frame table (`public/cad-frames.json`, `src/engine/cadFrames.js`):** for each CAD part number, how the library model sits relative to VEX's: by its box centre (`rule: "centre"`, most parts) or its own origin (`"origin"` with `offsetMm`), and a `turn`. `source` says how it was found: `pins` (checked against seated pins and holes in a real build, the 2026-27 Herobot Flop), `mesh` (matched to VEX's model of the part) or `default` (unchecked). A part number with no entry uses the box centre and is reported as unchecked.
- **`node scripts/fit-cad-frames.mjs <project> [--write] [--only pn,pn]`** downloads a project's archive once (`.cache/cad-projects/`), decodes its Draco part meshes and finds, for each part number, the quarter turn and offset that lay the library model on VEX's. It writes `mesh` entries whose mean distance is at most `0.5 mm` (never over a `pins` entry), lists poor matches, and says how each existing entry compares.

Pages and sections carry topic `tags:` and `answers:` claims (`src/help/tags.js`: `TOPIC_TAGS` and `SITUATIONS`); `helpContext` and `partKindTag` (`src/help/context.js`) work out the situation and `helpTarget` (`src/help/content.js`) picks the page or section. Related pages (up to three, most shared tags) end each page. Tool panels and dialogs have their own small **?** button (named "Help with <tool>").
- **Where things live:** pages in `src/help/pages/*.md` (writing rules in `src/help/WRITING.md`); the controls Show me can flash in `src/help/controls.js`; which features have a page in `src/help/coverage.json`; pictures in `public/help/shots/` with their marks in `shots.json`, made from the shot lists in `scripts/help-shots/shots-*.mjs`.
- **Checks:** `node scripts/check-help.mjs` (page rules, links, coverage, pictures), `node scripts/check-help-tags.mjs` (tags and claims), `node scripts/check-help-context.mjs` (situations).
- **Retake pictures:** build the preview (`VEXIQ_BASE=/vexiq-builder/ npm run build:static`), then `node scripts/capture-help-shots.mjs [name…]` (needs Chrome installed; it uses a new empty profile and does not touch the dev server).

## 6. OpenSpec Spec-Driven Development (SDD) Framework

- **AI-Native Spec-Driven Development (`openspec/`):**
  - Integrated via `@fission-ai/openspec` with multi-agent workflows configured in `.agents/`, `.claude/`, and `.cursor/`.
  - Directory structure:
    - `openspec/specs/`: Source-of-truth living project specifications.
    - `openspec/changes/`: Proposed change sets, delta specifications, designs, and task checklists.
    - `openspec/config.yaml`: Workflow schema configuration (`schema: spec-driven`).
  - Available slash workflows:
    - `/opsx-propose`: Create and align on structured proposals, delta specs, and tasks before writing code.
    - `/opsx-apply`: Apply and implement an approved change proposal.
    - `/opsx-archive`: Verify and archive completed changes into living project specs.
    - `/opsx-explore`: Explore ideas and codebase capabilities.
    - `/opsx-onboard`: Interactive onboarding for understanding existing specs.
    - `/opsx-sync`: Synchronize delta specs with main specs.
    - `/opsx-update`: Update and maintain existing change proposals.
