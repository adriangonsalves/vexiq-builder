Pins hold parts together, standoffs keep them apart, and corner connectors turn a join a quarter turn.

1. Count the layers you want to hold. A beam or plate is one layer.
2. Pick a pin with those layers in its name. A 1x1 pin holds one layer on each side.
3. Use a standoff when two parts must sit apart, such as a brain above a side plate.
4. Use a corner connector when two parts meet at a right angle.
   ![The Speedbot with its pins, standoffs and corner connectors](shot:pins-speedbot)
5. Join the part to its holes, as shown on [Joining parts](help:joining).

> **Watch out** Check which way a corner connector faces before you move on.

<!-- more -->

## Which pin holds how many layers
A pin's name has two numbers, like 1x2. Add them to get the layers it holds. A 1x1 pin holds two layers. A 1x2 holds three, a 2x2 holds four, and a 3x3 holds six. The 0x2 and 0x3 pins hold two and three.

Each layer is one beam or plate. Pick the pin that is just long enough, so little of it sticks out.

The Smart Motor mounts on four 1x1 pins. See [Shafts, wheels and motors](help:shafts-and-wheels).

## Standoffs
A standoff is a post with a pin at each end. Its name gives the length of the post in pitch. A 1x Pitch Standoff leaves one pitch between the parts, the gap between two holes. Longer standoffs leave more.

The brain's side plates hang from its side rails on 1x Pitch Standoffs. Corner connector pegs can do the same job.

## The two corner connectors and which way they face
The **Double 2x Wide, 1x2 Corner Connector** has a flat face with three holes in a clean rim. That face goes out. When it joins a side plate to the brain, the flat face points up. The ribbed, open side faces in.

The **2x Wide, 2x2 Offset Corner Connector** has its pegs to one side of the block. On the outside of a plate, the block wraps round the plate's end. That lines the pegs up with the plate's outer face.

## Checking a part that fits more than one way
Both ways of a corner connector fit the pins, so the app can't tell you which is right. The same goes for shafts with a stop ring, collars and hubs. Compare each part with the build instructions, from the same angle.

A part that goes in more than one way can be stepped through its other fits. See [Stepping through fits](help:joining#stepping-through-fits). A clean result in [Check and fix your build](help:check-and-fix) does not mean the parts face the right way.
